# Chapter 32 Study: Oral Health

## Summary

**Examine and prevent**
- Oral health supports eating, speech, sensation and social interaction; include oral screening in the general assessment (p453-454).
- Inspect palate, uvula, tongue, cheeks, lips, floor of mouth, gums and teeth with good lighting and retraction (p465).
- Report infection/abscess promptly; document loose, fractured, decayed or missing teeth and abnormal tissue in dental referrals (p465).
- Fluoride, effective brushing and interdental cleaning help prevent caries; adapt brush handles or use electric brushes when disability limits care (p456-457, p466).
- Periodontal disease may progress without pain; treatment depends on infection, bone loss and the person's overall health and function (p456-457).

**Dry mouth, senses and mucosa**
- Salivary hypofunction is not an inevitable consequence of healthy aging. Review medicines, systemic disease and head/neck treatment (p457-458).
- Address causative medicines where possible; consider dose modification, alternatives, sugar-free stimulation and saliva substitutes (p458).
- Pilocarpine **5 mg TID** or cevimeline **30 mg TID** can help when functional salivary tissue remains (p458).
- Oral moisture can be supported with appropriate sugar-free fluids and gels; keep removable dentures clean and out during sleep (p458).
- Taste changes in healthy aging are generally modest; medicines and impaired smell can contribute to food complaints (p458-459).
- Consider malignancy in persistent lesions: biopsy a lesion not healed within **2 to 3 weeks** after removal of suspected causes (p461).
- Before cancer therapy, arrange comprehensive dental assessment and treatment of infection; discuss treatment-related oral complications (p461).

**Function and dependent care**
- Dentition, disease and neuromuscular impairment affect mastication and swallowing; assess function as well as visible pathology (p461-462).
- Ask about pain and difficulty biting or chewing; financial and access barriers may prevent needed dental care (p463-464).
- In long-term care, provide reliable assisted hygiene, regular dental review and adaptations for disability (p465-466).
- Near life's end, priorities may shift toward comfort, safety, dignity and preserving function (p465).
