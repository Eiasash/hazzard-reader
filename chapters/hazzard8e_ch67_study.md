## Recognizing pain when dementia limits communication

<span data-exam-ids="mcq-795f2687afa4d5918ddb65de" data-exam-read-page="1048" data-reader-metadata="exam"></span>

Start with the patient. Someone who can point to a face or choose a number can still contribute a self-report. In June 2023 Q23, severe dementia, a stage 4 pressure ulcer and new agitation make observation particularly useful. PAINAD records breathing, negative vocalization, facial expression, body language and consolability. Observe the resident during movement or wound care and reassess after analgesia. Family observations help establish baseline behavior. A relative choosing a number cannot supply the patient's subjective rating. [PAINAD, p. 1048](?chapter=67#exam-p1048-mcq-795f2687afa4d5918ddb65de) · [self-report scales, p. 1059](?chapter=68#exam-p1059-mcq-795f2687afa4d5918ddb65de) · [original PAINAD instrument](https://aci.health.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/212921/Pain-assessment-advanced-dementia-scale.pdf).

Agitation has several possible causes. Identify the specific behavior, look for painful or environmental triggers, and see what changes when those triggers are removed. A nursing-home trial used a stepwise analgesic regimen, starting most residents on acetaminophen and escalating when pain persisted. Behavioral symptoms decreased. An antipsychotic is a last resort for persistent behavior that puts someone at risk after underlying causes and non-drug approaches have been addressed (pp. 1047–1048).

## Feeding and aspiration pneumonia in advanced dementia

<span data-exam-ids="mcq-caa086c8705cdeb3606c5536" data-exam-read-page="1048" data-reader-metadata="exam"></span>

**Intake and pressure injuries.** Check for ill-fitting dentures, oral ulceration, depression and medication effects when intake declines. Assisted oral feeding lets staff adapt the pace and texture. In advanced dementia, feeding tubes have not demonstrated improved survival, quality of life, nutritional measures or healing of pressure ulcers. New ulcers and use of restraints have been associated with tubes. Aspiration can still occur because patients continue to swallow saliva and may reflux stomach contents. June 2025 Q38 asks about *new* ulcers, a different outcome from healing an existing wound. [Feeding evidence, p. 1048](?chapter=67#exam-p1048-mcq-caa086c8705cdeb3606c5536).

<span data-exam-ids="mcq-8b2cfa55422f88f802142d29" data-exam-read-page="1048" data-reader-metadata="exam"></span>

**Antibiotics and the goal of treatment.** A resident with advanced dementia and aspiration pneumonia may live longer after antibiotics, without improved comfort. The survival association was similar across administration routes. Intravenous treatment or hospital transfer carried the greatest discomfort. Ask whether comfort or possible life prolongation takes priority and whether treatment in place could serve that aim. Treat dyspnea, pain and fever regardless of the antibiotic decision. In contrast, treatment of suspected urinary infection in a cohort with advanced dementia did not prolong survival. June 2025 Q63 describes pneumonia. [Infection outcomes, p. 1048](?chapter=67#exam-p1048-mcq-8b2cfa55422f88f802142d29).

**Plan before the next infection.** Dementia involves prolonged disability and can carry a physical symptom burden comparable to that of advanced cancer. Disability may conceal pain or dyspnea when self-report fails. Begin advance planning while the person can describe preferences. Later, help the surrogate anticipate progressive dysphagia, weight loss and infections. Irreversible weight loss, low BMI, advanced age and dependence support a limited prognosis, but an individual six-month estimate remains difficult (pp. 1047–1048, Table 67-1).

## Early palliative care alongside disease treatment

<span data-exam-ids="mcq-4ec2512c26a7775166980a4b" data-exam-read-page="1047" data-reader-metadata="exam"></span>

**Metastatic lung cancer.** At diagnosis, patients can receive symptom treatment, attention to mood and help with decisions alongside cancer therapy. In the randomized metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer trial, early-care patients had better quality of life, less depression and median survival of 11.6 months versus 8.9 months with usual care. Hazzard rounds the early-care median to 12 months. June 2026 Q62 asks about a possible survival benefit during chemotherapy and radiotherapy. The trial cannot predict the benefit for one patient or establish that every cancer population lives longer. [Hazzard, p. 1047](?chapter=67#exam-p1047-mcq-4ec2512c26a7775166980a4b) · [Temel trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20818875/).

**Referral before a crisis.** In a broader advanced-cancer trial, several quality-of-life differences took four months to emerge. ENABLE III reported one-year survival of 63% with early involvement and 48% after a three-month delay. In pooled noncancer trials, palliative interventions reduced symptom burden, hospital admissions and emergency visits. The dementia trials showed less consistent intervention benefits despite substantial need (p. 1047).

**Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.** In the June 2022 question, distinguish treatment of fibrosis from relief of distress. Antifibrotics can slow functional decline. Assess cough, breathlessness, planning and caregiver support from diagnosis and involve a specialist palliative team if needs exceed the usual team's resources. The Geriatrics Review Syllabus covers the pulmonary setting. [NICE QS79, pp. 19–21](?chapter=67#ch67-ipf).

## Israeli context — דמנציה, הזנה והקלה על סבל

**את מי הגדיר החוק?** קביעה של חולה הנוטה למות דורשת מחלה חשוכת מרפא ותוחלת חיים של עד שישה חודשים גם אם יינתן טיפול. בשלב הסופי נדרשים גם כשל של כמה מערכות חיוניות ותוחלת חיים של עד שבועיים. דמנציה כשלעצמה אינה מספיקה לקביעה. המלצות המותב עוסקות בחולה עם קיהיון שהוגדר כך לפי החוק. [חוק החולה הנוטה למות, סעיף 8](https://main.knesset.gov.il/apps/legislation/main/laws/2000296) · [המלצות המותב, עמ׳ 1–2](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/peg-dementia).

**בחירת דרך ההאכלה.** המותב מעדיף האכלה פומית כל עוד היא מתאימה מבחינה רפואית ותזונתית. מערבים קלינאית תקשורת ותזונאית, מתאימים את מרקם המזון ומאפשרים זמן להאכלה זהירה. כאשר לא ניתן להאכיל ולהשקות דרך הפה במידה הראויה חולה עם קיהיון הנוטה למות, המותב מנחה לספק מזון ונוזלים בדרך המתאימה למצב, לרבות השלמת נוזלים בעירוי, הזנה בזונדה או ב־PEG (עמ׳ 6, סעיף ח). אם נדרשת דרך הזנה ממושכת והיא אפשרית מבחינה רפואית, המותב מעדיף PEG על פני זונדה קבועה משיקולי נוחות. זונדה זמנית עשויה להתאים עד לקבלת החלטה. המותב ממליץ להימנע מהתקנת PEG בזמן מחלה חריפה בחולה זה ובשלב הסופי. הרופא האחראי מתעד את ההחלטה ואת נימוקיה. [המלצות מעשיות א–ט, עמ׳ 5–6](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/peg-dementia).

**השלב הסופי וטיפול מקל.** בחולה הנוטה למות שאינו כשיר, סובל סבל משמעותי ונקבע לפי החוק שאינו רוצה בהארכת חייו, סעיף 16 מבחין בין טיפול במחלה חשוכת המרפא לבין טיפול נלווה. כל עוד לא הוגדר בשלב הסופי, אין להימנע מטיפול נלווה, ובכללו מזון ונוזלים גם באמצעים מלאכותיים. אם הוגדר בשלב הסופי ומתקיימים אותם תנאים, סעיף 17 מורה להימנע בהתאם לרצונו גם מטיפול נלווה, למעט נוזלים. יש להמשיך מתן נוזלים גם באמצעים מלאכותיים, אלא אם הרופא האחראי קבע שהנוזלים גורמים סבל או נזק. סעיף 23 מחייב לעשות ככל האפשר להקל על כאב וסבל לפי רצון החולה, גם אם כרוך בכך סיכון סביר לחייו. [חוק החולה הנוטה למות, סעיפים 16(ב), 17 ו־23](https://main.knesset.gov.il/apps/legislation/main/laws/2000296).

## Hospice, frailty and care for the whole person

**Primary and specialist care.** Any clinician can treat common symptoms, coordinate care, discuss future decisions and support caregivers. Complex or refractory needs justify interprofessional specialist involvement. Hazzard describes US Medicare hospice eligibility as an expected prognosis **less than 6 months** on the usual disease course, certified by **the attending physician and hospice medical director** (p1046). Services can include medications for the terminal illness, equipment, respite and bereavement support (pp1045–1047).

> Current practice (not the book): CMS uses **6 months or less**. Initial certification requires the hospice medical director or hospice interdisciplinary-group physician and the patient’s attending physician **if any** — [Medicare Benefit Policy Manual, ch 9, §20.1](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/r13664bp.pdf).

**Why trajectory matters.** A person who reached age 65 in the 2019 US life tables could on average expect nearly 20 more years (Figure 67-1). Years of disability and illness can precede death, and changes of setting late in life can fragment care. In hospice studies, patients enrolled for more than a month tended to experience better outcomes. Prognostic uncertainty should prompt earlier conversations; it need not delay routine relief of distress (pp. 1046–1048).

**Frailty and treatment feasibility.** Before surgery, early frailty can respond to resistance training and correction of nutritional inadequacy. In severe frailty, diminished reserve may limit or reverse the benefit of intensive rehabilitation. Define a functional goal, try an appropriate intervention and revisit goals when the patient does not improve. Modifying the home or arranging help may better preserve an important activity. Multiple irreversible conditions can support US hospice eligibility even though frailty alone cannot serve as the principal hospice billing diagnosis (pp. 1048–1049, Table 67-2).

**Diversity and support.** Ask who gives the patient practical and emotional support, what name and pronouns they use, and whom they want involved in decisions. Families of choice can be critical to both care and bereavement. Help the patient document a preferred surrogate within the relevant legal framework. A trained interpreter can better protect the person's voice when family members might omit sensitive information (pp. 1049–1051, Table 67-3).

## Prognosis, advance planning and spiritual care

**Estimate, communicate, then check interpretation.** Combine stage of disease, function, cognition and comorbidity. Clinicians often overestimate survival. Discuss a range or several plausible scenarios, after asking what the patient wants to know. Ask the patient or surrogate what they took from the discussion; their experience of previous recoveries can lead them to interpret the same facts differently. Compare time to benefit with life expectancy before recommending preventive treatment (pp. 1050–1052).

**Decisions across settings.** For multimorbidity, start with patient preferences, assess how well the evidence fits, consider prognosis and feasibility, and simplify the plan around those priorities. Revisit the plan as circumstances change. In the US, a health care power of attorney names the future decision maker, a living will records preferences for future decisions after capacity is lost, and POLST translates current choices into portable medical orders. A videoconference can include a distant supporter if the patient wishes. Check hearing and visual cues and summarize the plan afterward (pp. 1049, 1052–1053, Table 67-4).

**CPR and spiritual distress.** ICARE starts by introducing the discussion, then elicits care goals, checks alignment, makes a recommendation and explores apparent conflicts. The chapter reports 17% survival to discharge and 7% discharge home among older inpatients undergoing CPR (p1053). Relate the likely outcome to what matters to this individual. FICA explores faith or other sources of meaning, their importance, community, and how to address those needs in care. Offer spiritual or community support in the form the person chooses (pp. 1053–1054, Table 67-5).

## Summary

### Definition & scope
- Palliative care addresses serious-illness symptoms, stress and quality of life for patients and families; it can accompany curative treatment at any stage. Primary palliative care includes symptom care, coordination, communication and caregiver support. (p. 1045)
- Hazzard describes an expected prognosis **less than six months** if the illness runs its usual course, certified by **the attending physician and hospice medical director** (p1046). The same page describes CMS coverage guidance for a prognosis of six months or less.
  > Current practice (not the book): CMS uses **six months or less**; for the initial period, the hospice physician and the designated attending physician, **if any**, certify — [CMS, chapter 9, §20.1](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/r13664bp.pdf).
<a id="p1047"></a>
- Trials support quality-of-life and family benefits; noncancer evidence includes less emergency use, hospitalization and symptom burden. Dementia trials have been least promising. (p. 1047)

### Assessment & planning
- Prognostication involves estimating, communicating and interpreting prognosis; compare time to benefit with life expectancy. (p. 1050)
- Clinicians tend to overestimate survival by **3-5-fold**. Combine clinical information, cognition, function and prognostic indices; acknowledge uncertainty. (p. 1051)
- ACP is an ongoing conversation. In the US framework, a health care proxy document designates a decision maker, a living will states future wishes, and POLST records current treatment orders. (p. 1052)

### Management & drugs
<a id="p1048"></a>
- Advanced dementia: offer assisted oral feeding; feeding tubes have not improved survival, quality of life, aspiration prevention or pressure-ulcer healing. (p. 1048)
- Pain may drive behavioral symptoms: acetaminophen was the first step for most patients in a stepwise trial; opioids may be tried for nonresponders. Antipsychotics are not first-line behavioral treatment. (p. 1048)
- Aspiration-pneumonia antibiotics in advanced dementia were associated with longer survival, not better comfort; intravenous treatment or hospitalization was associated with the greatest discomfort. Align infection treatment with goals. (p. 1048)
- Frailty care: consider reversibility, treatment burden and patient goals; a therapeutic trial can clarify potential, with renewed goals discussion if function does not improve. (p. 1049)

### Exam traps
- Palliative care does not require stopping curative therapy. (p. 1045)
- Frailty's exclusion as a principal US hospice billing diagnosis does not itself exclude hospice eligibility. (p. 1049)
- ACP is more than a completed form; preferences may change. (p. 1052)

## Recall

**2. What distinguishes primary from specialist palliative care?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>All clinicians can provide core symptom management, coordination, goals discussions and caregiver support; specialists contribute an interprofessional team with palliative expertise. (p. 1045)</details>

**5. What did the noncancer palliative-care meta-analysis associate with palliative care?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Less emergency department use, hospitalization and symptom burden; dementia trials had been least promising. (p. 1047)</details>

**6. What should precede medication for dementia-related behavioral symptoms?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Identify the behavior and severity, identify and remove physiologic/environmental triggers when possible, and attempt nonpharmacologic interventions. (p. 1047)</details>

**7. What pain-treatment approach reduced behavioral symptoms in a nursing-home trial?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An empiric stepwise protocol; most patients received acetaminophen at the first step. Opioids may be tried for nonresponders, and PAINAD may help assessment. (p. 1048)</details>

**9. How did aspiration-pneumonia antibiotics affect survival and comfort in advanced dementia?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They were associated with improved survival, not improved comfort; intravenous treatment or hospitalization was associated with the greatest discomfort, with similar survival benefit across administration routes. (p. 1048)</details>

**11. What domains structure care for multimorbidity?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Patient preferences, interpretation of evidence, prognosis, clinical feasibility, and optimization of therapies/care plans. (p. 1049)</details>

**14. How can a clinician express prognostic uncertainty?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Give a time range or best-case, worst-case and most common scenarios; support emotional reactions with empathy and therapeutic silence. (p. 1052)</details>

**15. In the chapter's US framework, how do proxy, living will and POLST differ?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A health care proxy names a future decision maker; a living will records future treatment wishes; POLST is a standing medical order for current care that accompanies the patient across settings. (p. 1052)</details>
