# Behavioral Symptoms of Dementia and Psychoactive Drug Therapy

Identify what the behavior means to the patient before trying to suppress it. Pain, delirium, medicines and an unmet need can look similar.

## Behavioral syndromes and assessment

Depression, anxiety, apathy, hallucinations and disinhibition can emerge before or after cognitive decline becomes obvious. The chapter links these symptoms to vulnerability in brain circuits controlling behavior, compounded by illness, pain, sensory deficits and the demands of the surroundings. Their presence is associated with faster decline, greater caregiver burden and a greater likelihood of institutional placement. Improvement in one target symptom does not establish improvement in cognition or independence. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p919">pp. 919</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p920">920</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#p927">927</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p928">928</a>.

Neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPS) occur in up to **98%** of dementia patients over the course of illness (<a href="?chapter=60#p919">p919</a>); depression, apathy, and anxiety are the most common NPS in dementia (<a href="?chapter=60#p919">p919</a>).

New late-life NPS lasting **over 6 months**, without dementia and not explained by another psychiatric disorder = mild behavioral impairment (MBI) (<a href="?chapter=60#p919">p919</a>).

NPS are associated with higher mortality, faster cognitive decline and institutional placement (<a href="?chapter=60#p919">pp. 919</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p920">920</a>).

**Get the time course from someone who knows the person.** Years of decline with wandering or social disinhibition can fit dementia. Marked new sleepiness over two days or rapidly changing agitation warrants an assessment for delirium, including attention, arousal, medication changes, pain and acute illness. Profound sadness and suicidal thoughts require separate urgent assessment. Use examination and focused tests to investigate plausible causes. The NPI-Q can describe symptoms and caregiver distress but cannot identify their cause. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p920">pp. 920</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p923">923</a>.

**Ask what the hallucination means to this patient.** Formed visions of people or animals, cognitive fluctuations, parkinsonism and dream enactment favor dementia with Lewy bodies. A calm person seeing something others cannot see may need reassurance and observation. Distressing or dangerous symptoms need further evaluation. Review agents such as amantadine that can worsen hallucinations. Donepezil or another cholinesterase inhibitor may help cognition and hallucinations. Dopamine-blocking antipsychotics can cause profound rigidity or a severe sensitivity reaction. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p923">pp. 923</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p924">924</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#p928">928</a> and <a href="?chapter=63#p984">984</a>.

4 NPS domains: affective/motivational, psychotic, disturbances of basic drives (sleep/eating), and disinhibited/socially inappropriate behavior.

## Depression, apathy and other mimics

### Separate major depression, apathy and medication toxicity

Crying, withdrawal, irritability and new anxiety can represent depression, but apathy and cognitive decline can produce overlapping symptoms. Check duration, functional effect, enjoyment, sleep, appetite and suicidal thoughts with the patient and informant. A screening score signals the need for assessment. An SSRI such as escitalopram can target a depressive disorder when medication is warranted. Donepezil targets symptomatic Alzheimer disease. The June 2023 item accepted both choices. Antidepressant benefit for depression specifically in dementia is inconsistent, and NICE favors psychological approaches for mild to moderate depression in mild to moderate dementia. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p923">pp. 923</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#p928">928</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p931">931</a>. NICE NG97, recommendations 1.7.11–1.7.12.

**Rule out delirium first** for any acute or fluctuating change in cognition/behavior (<a href="?chapter=60#p923">p923</a>).

Depression affects up to **50%** of dementia patients and may go undetected — in dementia it is more likely to present with agitation, irritability, and anxiety (<a href="?chapter=60#p923">p923</a>).

Psychosis occurs in **20% to 30%** of AD patients vs **over 50%** of DLB/Parkinson disease dementia patients; visual hallucinations (more common in DLB) may be the most clinically useful feature to distinguish DLB from AD (<a href="?chapter=60#p923">p923</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p924">924</a>).

For an older adult with major depression, mild anxiety and ischemic heart disease, sertraline is a reasonable first drug. It has little anticholinergic activity. Bupropion may be activating, and comorbid kidney disease calls for dose caution. Amitriptyline adds orthostasis and anticholinergic and cardiac effects. An antipsychotic is a poor initial choice for uncomplicated major depression. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p929">pp. 929</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p930">930</a>.

**Think in mechanisms.** Confusion with urinary retention suggests muscarinic blockade: paroxetine stands out among commonly used SSRIs, while escitalopram has relatively little anticholinergic activity. Amitriptyline and promethazine also add substantial burden. New confusion alone might reflect hyponatremia. Agitation accompanied by clonus, hyperreflexia and autonomic disturbance instead suggests serotonin toxicity, which can occur with citalopram especially alongside another serotonergic drug. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p922">pp. 922</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#p929">929</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p930">930</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-3">Tables 60-3</a> and <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-9">60-9</a>. AGS Beers Criteria 2023, <a href="?chapter=laws&amp;lawCard=ima-article-beers-2023#law-card-ima-article-beers-2023">Table 7</a>.

NPI = most-used research tool for NPS; NPI-Q = brief clinic version, measures **12 NPS** domains plus informant distress (<a href="?chapter=60#p922">p922</a>).

Medication review is essential: the anticholinergic-risk/delirium med list (**<a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-3">Table 60-3</a>**, <a href="?chapter=60#p922">p922</a>) includes non-obvious agents **digoxin** and **furosemide** alongside the classic anticholinergics (TCAs, diphenhydramine/hydroxyzine/promethazine, oxybutynin, scopolamine).

New-onset acute NPS work-up: CBC, chem panel, LFTs, UA/culture; consider TSH, B12, folate, tox screen, ECG; chest x-ray if respiratory signs; imaging particularly if focal signs; EEG if seizure suspected; CSF rarely needed — indicated if meningitis/encephalitis suspected (<a href="?chapter=60#p922">p922</a>). Interpret urine findings in context. With delirium and bacteriuria alone, and no urinary symptoms, fever or hemodynamic instability, assess other causes and observe rather than treating bacteriuria. [IDSA, recommendation V.1](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/asymptomatic-bacteriuria/).

SSRIs and SNRIs can lower serum sodium through inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion. Consider the drug and other contributors if new confusion or gait instability follows initiation. Both mirtazapine and bupropion were accepted as lower-risk options in the September item. Neither guarantees normal sodium. Mirtazapine can increase appetite and daytime sleepiness, even at a low dose. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p929">pp. 929</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p930">930</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-9">Table 60-9</a>.

**Read the ECG from the beginning of QRS to the end of T.** Citalopram prolongs ventricular repolarization in a dose-dependent manner. <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-9">Table 60-9</a> gives a maximum of 20 mg daily above age 60. Hypokalemia and other QT-prolonging drugs increase concern. Hazzard describes improved agitation, lower caregiver stress, better ADL performance and less rescue lorazepam with citalopram in CitAD (<a href="?chapter=60#p929">p929</a>).

> **Current practice: Citalopram benefits and QT effects.** the published CitAD trial found improvement in selected agitation and caregiver-distress measures, with cognitive worsening and QT prolongation at its target of 30 mg/day. ADL performance and rescue lorazepam use did **not** differ significantly. [CitAD trial, 2014, Table 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24549548/) · [S-CitAD trial, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12951298/).

**Lithium and a new thiazide.** Sodium loss can reduce renal lithium clearance. New tremor, falls, altered consciousness or clonus warrant prompt evaluation of lithium level, electrolytes and kidney function. A routine urine toxicology screen does not measure lithium. For agitation in Alzheimer disease, the completed Lit-AD trial found no significant advantage over placebo on its primary agitation and aggression measure. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p931">p. 931</a>. [Lithium prescribing information, sections 5.1 and 7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=7dc9c6d2-6d9a-49e4-a8ab-437b0ed5f84e&type=display) · [Lit-AD results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34059401/)

Get a reliable informant history — the patient often cannot self-report accurately (<a href="?chapter=60#p920">p920</a>).

## Care interactions and non-drug treatment

During a difficult bath, ask the caregiver to describe the moment that went wrong, what preceded it, what the person did and how each responded. **Investigate** means looking for a cause that can change: painful movement, constipation, cold, fear, loss of privacy, unfamiliar instructions or a medication effect. **Create** a plan with the person, caregiver and team: try a familiar time, offer a choice, give one instruction at a time and pause when fear rises. **Evaluate** asks both whether the plan was tried and whether distress decreased. If the plan was impractical, find out why. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p924">p. 924</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-4">Table 60-4</a>.

The June 2025 bathing vignette cites the International Psychogeriatric Association article. Its cycle is Investigate, Plan and Act. It discusses DICE as a compatible method. Someone who can still be reassured during dressing or bathing gives the team a reason to investigate that interaction before adding a sedative. See <a href="?chapter=bank&amp;q=mcq-9fd9a6436651576bca84dab2">Figure 1</a> and the Investigation section of the IPA consensus article, and Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p924">pp. 924</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p926">926</a> for related teaching.

**Support the caregiver as part of the intervention.** In-home occupational assessment shows which tasks the person can do with cues and which require supervision. Identify who can relieve a caregiver who is losing sleep. A respite service provides substitute care for a defined period. A safety plan for someone who wanders must work even when the person cannot ask for help. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p924">pp. 924</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p926">926</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-5">Tables 60-5</a> and <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-6">60-6</a>.

**Nonpharmacologic interventions are first-line** — exception is emergency situations where the safety of the patient or others is compromised (eg, severe agitation) (<a href="?chapter=60#p925">p925</a>).

Psychotropics only after nonpharmacologic approaches fail, EXCEPT 3 cases: clear-cut major depression; psychosis causing/risking harm; aggression causing/risking harm (<a href="?chapter=60#p925">p925</a>).

Psychotropics are routinely prescribed despite safety/efficacy concerns: in newly admitted nursing-home residents, only **12%** of new admissions got nonpharmacologic treatment in the first 3 months, while **71%** received at least one psychotropic (<a href="?chapter=60#p927">p927</a>).

## Medicines, benefits and harms

### Decide whether an antipsychotic trial is justified

Clarify the target: a distressing delusion, repeated physical injuries or an immediate threat changes the decision more than a nonspecific report of “agitation.” Treat delirium and other reversible causes and adapt the setting. NICE permits an antipsychotic when there is risk of harm or severe distress from agitation, hallucinations or delusions. Family distress also calls for support, but burden alone is insufficient. Explain the likely size of the benefit and the risks to the patient and caregiver. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p925">pp. 925</a> and <a href="?chapter=60#p927">927</a>. NICE NG97, recommendations 1.7.1–1.7.8.

**Define success and when to stop.** For example, fewer episodes of hitting during necessary care with preserved wakefulness is a meaningful response. Start one medication at a low dose and titrate toward the minimum effective dose. Watch gait, alertness, orthostatic symptoms, stiffness, cognition and function. After four weeks at an adequate dose without significant benefit, taper and withdraw. Assess at least monthly during tapering and for at least four months afterward. For ongoing treatment, NICE calls for reassessment at least every six weeks. Continue behavioral care throughout. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p926">pp. 926</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p928">928</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-7">Table 60-7</a>.

No significant benefit after a **4-week period** → taper and withdraw; reassess at least monthly while tapering and for at least **4 months** after stopping (<a href="?chapter=60#p927">p927</a>).

**Clozapine, 6.25–75 mg/day.** Hazzard calls clozapine a good option for psychotic symptoms in DLB because it has a low risk of extrapyramidal effects. Neutropenia or agranulocytosis limits its use. The table specifies weekly blood draws during the first **6 months** (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>).

> **Current practice: Clozapine in Lewy body dementia.** The DLB Consortium cites benefit in Parkinson disease psychosis. Efficacy and tolerability in DLB itself remain unestablished. [Fourth consensus report, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5496518/).

**Recognize drug-specific harms.** In dementia, antipsychotic exposure increases mortality, including cardiovascular deaths. Haloperidol has substantial extrapyramidal risk, can lower the seizure threshold and can cause orthostatic hypotension. Olanzapine can increase appetite, weight and glucose, with antimuscarinic effects at higher doses. Quetiapine can cause sedation, orthostasis and antimuscarinic effects at higher doses. The June 2022 anticholinergic item gave credit for every choice. It supplies no reliable potency ranking. Hazzard <a href="?chapter=60#p927">pp. 927</a>–<a href="?chapter=60#p928">928</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-8">Table 60-8</a>.

Risperidone **0.25–1 mg**/day — most effective for psychosis and agitation per AHRQ review; dose-dependent EPS, ↑cerebrovascular risk (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>).

Olanzapine **2.5–10 mg**/day — most effective for agitation per AHRQ review; sedation, weight gain, glucose effects (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>).

Quetiapine **12.5–150 mg**/day — sedation, orthostatic hypotension; with clozapine, less likely than other antipsychotics to worsen parkinsonian symptoms (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>).

Aripiprazole **5–10 mg**/day — most effective for overall NPS per AHRQ review; lower EPS, nausea (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>).

Haloperidol **0.5–5** mg/day — high EPS risk; should not be a first-line neuroleptic in nonemergent situations (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>). Retrospective cohorts: higher mortality than risperidone, olanzapine, aripiprazole, ziprasidone, or quetiapine (<a href="?chapter=60#p927">p927</a>).

Pimavanserin **34 mg**/day — FDA-approved for Parkinson disease psychosis; confusion, edema, hypotension (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>)

> **Current practice: Reconciling the Parkinson psychosis doses.** Table 60-8 agrees with the current US dose. Chapter 61 gives an older 17-mg schedule on [p946](?chapter=61#p946). The current dose is 34 mg **once daily**, reduced to 10 mg once daily with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor. [Nuplazid label, 2026](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1e6bea44-57d6-4bac-9328-46e1ee59f83b).

> **Current practice: Brexpiprazole indications.** brexpiprazole received FDA approval in May 2023 for agitation associated with Alzheimer dementia; its boxed warning states increased mortality in older patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotics - AGS Alternatives 2025, <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40697073/">Table 3</a> footnote e, article p12; FDA announcement, 11 May 2023, approval and boxed-warning paragraphs

Paroxetine — anticholinergic properties, should be avoided in older adults (<a href="?chapter=60#p930">p930</a>); anticholinergic effects may increase delirium risk, and may cause withdrawal effects due to short half-life (<a href="?chapter=60#p929">p929</a>).

Nortriptyline — therapeutic window **50–150 ng/mL**; anticholinergic effects, hypotension, sedation (<a href="?chapter=60#p929">p929</a>).

**US approvals after publication.** Brexpiprazole was approved in 2023 for agitation associated with dementia due to Alzheimer disease and carries the antipsychotic boxed mortality warning. In 2026 the FDA approved extended-release dextromethorphan with bupropion for the same indication. Both are scheduled treatments. These indications do not generalize to isolated wandering or all types of dementia. [FDA brexpiprazole approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-treat-agitation-symptoms-associated-dementia-due-alzheimers-disease) · [FDA Auvelity label, sections 1.2 and 2.3](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215430s009s018lbl.pdf)

For significant delirium-related agitation requiring short-term medication, the chapter gives these low PRN doses at six-hour intervals (<a href="?chapter=60#p923">p923</a>). Haloperidol **0.25–0.5 mg** orally (IV only with appropriate monitoring), quetiapine **12.5–25 mg** orally, olanzapine **2.5 mg** orally or **1.25–2.5 mg** IM, or risperidone **0.25–0.5 mg orally**. There is no approved IV risperidone formulation.

> **Current practice: Risperidone route.** risperidone is not given IV; Risperdal Consta is IM only — "Do not administer intravenously" — Risperdal Consta prescribing information (Janssen).

Citalopram — initial **5–10** mg, max **20** mg/day; CitAD trial showed benefit for agitation, but QTc prolongation and worsened cognition may limit its use (<a href="?chapter=60#p929">p929</a>).

Sertraline — initial **25** mg, max **200** mg/day; despite early promise, not shown superior to placebo for depression in dementia (<a href="?chapter=60#p929">p929</a>).

Escitalopram is the S-enantiomer of citalopram. Hazzard describes S-CitAD as ongoing at press time (<a href="?chapter=60#p930">p930</a>).

> **Current practice: Escitalopram for agitation.** S-CitAD subsequently found no significant benefit for agitation at 12 weeks and observed drug-related QT prolongation. [S-CitAD trial, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12951298/).

Mirtazapine — sedating at lower doses; in the sleep pilot, 8 received mirtazapine and 16 placebo (24 total). Nighttime sleep did not improve and daytime sleepiness increased. <a href="?chapter=60#p930">Page 930</a> misstates the treatment-group size. [Scoralick et al., methods and results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26818096/).

> **Current practice: Mirtazapine for agitation.** In SYMBAD, mirtazapine did not improve agitation more than placebo in dementia. More deaths occurred in the mirtazapine group, an exploratory safety signal. Avoid using it solely as a presumed safer treatment for agitation. [SYMBAD trial, 2021](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=7727fe84-f6cc-4b31-bc00-1ecf741ad361).

Bupropion — avoid with a history of seizures or psychotic symptoms (<a href="?chapter=60#p930">p930</a>).

Desipramine — the other preferred TCA in <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-9">Table 60-9</a>; therapeutic window **150–300 ng/mL** (higher than nortriptyline's — don't conflate the two), similar side-effect profile (<a href="?chapter=60#p929">p929</a>).

> **Current practice: Secondary-amine tricyclics in older adults.** Nortriptyline and desipramine are both on the 2023 Beers list of antidepressants with strong anticholinergic activity. Sedation and orthostatic hypotension are additional concerns when considering either agent in dementia. [AGS Beers Criteria, Table 2, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12478568/).

TCAs overall are limited by a high adverse-event burden compared to SSRIs/SNRIs (<a href="?chapter=60#p930">p930</a>).

Benzodiazepines are used in **8.5% to 20%** of AD patients despite weak efficacy evidence; avoid, except emergencies (severe agitation with risk of harm) or failure of alternative pharmacologic interventions; if used, sparingly, short-acting preferred; glucuronidated agents (oxazepam, temazepam, lorazepam) preferable with complex medical comorbidities — no active metabolites (<a href="?chapter=60#p931">p931</a>).

Meta-analyses: donepezil, galantamine, and memantine beat placebo in reducing emergence of NPS (modest vs neuroleptics); AChI + memantine combination beats monotherapy and placebo; not first-line for acute NPS of moderate or greater severity, but reasonable for chronic NPS (<a href="?chapter=60#p931">p931</a>).

Methylphenidate improved apathy in AD in randomized trials; modafinil did not in one small RCT (<a href="?chapter=60#p931">p931</a>).

A Cochrane review found low-certainty evidence that melatonin doses **up to 10 mg** may have little or no sleep benefit (<a href="?chapter=60#p931">p931</a>); one small trial of melatonin **3 mg** helped agitation/sundowning (<a href="?chapter=60#p932">p932</a>); ramelteon showed no sleep benefit (<a href="?chapter=60#p932">p932</a>).

Anticonvulsants ("mood stabilizers") are generally disappointing: valproate is ineffective and poorly tolerated; carbamazepine helped in one small trial but is a potent enzyme inducer with marrow-toxicity risk (<a href="?chapter=60#p930">p930</a>).

**Pitfall.** The drug tables give US doses and availability at publication (<a href="?chapter=60#p928">p928</a>).

**Pitfall.** The chapter's own text lists cholinesterase-inhibitor side effects as "eight loss" — a printing error for "weight loss" (correctly spelled in <a href="?chapter=60#book-table-60-9">Table 60-9</a>, <a href="?chapter=60#p929">p929</a>); <a href="?chapter=60#p931">p931</a> uses the misspelling.

## Key numbers

- MIND at Home study: **99%** of community-dwelling dementia patients had unmet care needs (<a href="?chapter=60#p924">p924</a>); **60% to 70%** of older adults with AD and other dementias live in the community, cared for by family and friends (<a href="?chapter=60#p924">p924</a>).

- In the US about **25%** of people with dementia receive care from nonfamily caregivers (eg, home health aides), who may not always receive the necessary dementia-care training (<a href="?chapter=60#p925">p925</a>).
