# Chapter 93 Study: Skin Cancer

## Summary

**Definition & risk**
- Skin cancer is the most common US malignancy; cumulative ultraviolet exposure is a major risk. Most cases in older adults are basal or squamous cell carcinomas (p1455).
- Actinic keratoses are precancerous epidermal lesions on sun-exposed skin. Not every lesion progresses to squamous cell carcinoma (p1456, p1458).
- Melanoma and Merkel cell carcinoma are more aggressive, with greater nodal and distant metastatic potential (p1455).

**Assessment**
- Biopsy lesions suspicious for keratinocyte carcinoma; assess site, size, histology and recurrence risk when choosing treatment (p1460–1462).
- Melanoma clues: asymmetry, irregular borders, varied color and evolution; an “ugly duckling” unlike the patient's other lesions deserves attention (p1464).
- Diameter above **6 mm** alone is relatively insensitive. Small lesions can still be concerning if dark or changing (p1464).
- Suspected melanoma needs complete full-thickness excision with narrow **2-mm** margins or deep saucerization; superficial shaving can prevent accurate depth measurement (p1467).

**Management & drugs**
- Isolated actinic keratoses: liquid-nitrogen cryotherapy. High-risk keratinocyte cancers commonly require Mohs surgery with margin control (p1460–1462).
- Topical fluorouracil: for actinic keratoses, once daily for up to **4 weeks**; imiquimod: twice weekly for **16 weeks**. Both can cause irritation, dryness and burning (p1461).
- The **5%** formulations can treat selected biopsy-confirmed low-risk superficial basal cell cancers; their cancer dosing differs from actinic-keratosis dosing (p1461).
- Definitive melanoma management incorporates depth and nodal assessment; sentinel node biopsy is ideally coordinated with definitive excision when indicated (p1467–1468).
- Explain surgical recovery, reconstruction, treatment visits and functional demands; age alone should not determine treatment (p1470–1471).

**Exam traps**
- Basal cell carcinoma rarely metastasizes but can cause local morbidity; morpheaform lesions may extend beyond visible borders and recur (p1458).
- Near the end of life, active nonintervention may be reasonable for asymptomatic low-risk disease; palliative surgery may still help painful, bleeding or malodorous lesions (p1471).
