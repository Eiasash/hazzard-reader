# Chapter 3 Study: Immunology and Inflammation

> Excluded from the IMA required reading list (P005-2026). Cited in the 2020-22 past papers (Hazzard 7e, linked by chapter).

## Summary

**Immune aging is not uniform suppression**
- Immunosenescence combines low-grade baseline inflammation with impaired responses to acute stimuli; responses to new antigens are often more impaired than memory responses (p47).
- Hematopoietic stem-cell numbers may increase while differentiation and self-renewal deteriorate; output shifts toward myeloid cells at the expense of lymphoid progenitors (p47-48).
- Innate cells recognize pathogens and activate antigen presentation. Aging alters neutrophil, monocyte, dendritic-cell and natural-killer-cell functions, with both impaired responses and persistent inflammation (p48-52).
- Inflammaging involves raised inflammatory mediators and associations with mortality and age-related illness; associations do not prove that every mediator is a causal treatment target (p47, p50).

**Adaptive immunity**
- Antigen-receptor rearrangement creates diverse B/T repertoires; antigen selection supports specificity and memory (p52-54, p58).
- Thymic involution reduces new T-cell output. Fewer naive cells, repertoire contraction and accumulated differentiated/memory cells impair responses to new challenges (p55-57).
- Human marrow B-cell production appears generally preserved, unlike prominent mouse changes; circulating B-cell numbers, antibody responses and repertoire diversity decline (p58-59).

**Vaccines and investigational approaches in the book**
- Influenza high-dose/adjuvanted formulations can improve responses. One trial found **24% relative efficacy** for high-dose versus standard-dose vaccine against influenza-like illness (p60).
- Recombinant zoster vaccine reduced shingles by **97%** in a trial of adults **50 or older**, and **90%** in another of adults **70 or older**; the latter also reduced postherpetic neuralgia (p61).
- Pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine is largely T-independent; conjugation enables T-cell help and memory. CAPiTA's Dutch results may not transfer directly to US settings with childhood conjugate vaccination (p61).
- Coronavirus disease disproportionately affected older adults; inflammatory and immune-response changes are under investigation, with heterogeneous clinical studies (p61-62).
- Low-dose rapamycin/mTOR inhibition, senolytics and metformin are discussed as research approaches; the chapter does not establish routine anti-aging prescriptions (p60, p62).
