# Chapter 94 Study: Aging of the Hematopoietic System and Anemia

## Summary

**Hematopoietic aging**
- Aging brings impaired stem-cell function, greater clonal hematopoiesis and myeloid skewing with reduced lymphopoiesis; stress responses and immunity deteriorate (p1473).
- Intrinsic DNA/epigenetic/metabolic changes interact with the aging marrow niche and chronic inflammation (p1476–1479).
- Defective stress-induced hematopoiesis may be inapparent at baseline; anemia should prompt investigation for underlying disease (p1479–1480).

**Definition & causes**
- WHO thresholds used here are hemoglobin below **13 g/dL** in men and **12 g/dL** in women (p1479).
- Anemia affects over **10%** of community-dwelling adults at least **65**, and over **20%** at least **85**; even mild anemia is associated with adverse outcomes (p1479).
- Consider iron deficiency, inflammation, kidney disease, folate/cobalamin deficiency, myelodysplasia and other hematologic malignancy (p1480).
- Unexplained anemia accounts for **34–44%** in different studies, but more extensive work-up can reveal causes such as insufficient erythropoietin production (p1480).

**Assessment**
- Initial tests include CBC/differential, reticulocytes, ferritin/iron studies, creatinine, cobalamin, red-cell folate, thyroid and liver tests (p1480).
- If unrevealing, selectively assess erythropoietin, copper, zinc, inflammatory/hemolysis markers and serum electrophoresis (p1480).
- Suspected marrow malignancy requires marrow aspiration/biopsy to assess disorders such as myelodysplasia or leukemia (p1480).

**Management & drugs**
- Oral iron is usually sufficient for iron deficiency. The chapter favors alternate-day dosing because iron-induced hepcidin can inhibit next-day absorption (p1480).
- Intravenous iron is an alternative for intolerance or inadequate response. Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents have established uses in end-stage renal disease and selected myelodysplasia, but limited data in unexplained anemia (p1480).
- For acute symptomatic anemia, the chapter cites transfusion thresholds of **7 g/dL** generally and **8 g/dL** with end-organ ischemia symptoms, individualized for comorbidity and baseline state (p1480).

**Exam traps**
- Clonal mutations do not explain most unexplained anemia; increased frequency in anemia is only a small difference from controls (p1480).
- Treatment burden matters: frequent visits or transfusions without quality-of-life gain can harm frail patients. A threshold is not a universal target for every chronic anemia (p1480).
