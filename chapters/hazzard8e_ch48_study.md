# Chapter 48 Study: Elder Mistreatment

## Summary

**Recognize the spectrum**
- Mistreatment includes physical, sexual or psychological abuse, neglect, abandonment and financial exploitation; definitions vary (p719).
- It may occur at home, in the community or in institutions, often within a relationship of trust (p719).
- Studies suggest **as many as 10%** of community-dwelling older adults are affected annually; smaller studies suggest nearly **50%** among people with dementia (p720).
- Research suggests authorities identify **as few as 1 in 24** cases. Fear, dependence, isolation or cognitive impairment may prevent disclosure (p720).

**Clinical assessment**
- Risk factors are neither a diagnosis nor a reliable way to exclude mistreatment; family circumstances and perpetrator–victim relationships matter (p720–721).
- Chronic disease may mimic abuse, and abuse may be misattributed to chronic disease (p722).
- Maintain clinical suspicion; examine injuries, hygiene, nutrition, medications, function, cognition and social circumstances (p722–724).
- The suspected perpetrator's presence can complicate assessment; obtain appropriate expertise and collateral information sensitively (p722–723).

**Safety & response**
- Establish whether there is immediate danger, then make a practical safety plan even when danger is not immediate (p723–724).
- In most U.S. states, reporting to adult protective services is required; available services and statutory arrangements vary (p724).
- If the patient lacks capacity, document objective evidence; guardianship may be needed with protective/community services (p724–725).
- A capable person may choose to remain in an abusive environment: explain escalation risk, review safety options and remain available (p725).
- Use individualized, trauma-informed management. The chapter reports no good-quality randomized trials establishing an intervention strategy (p723, p725).

**Institutional settings**
- Consider neglect and failures of care planning, as well as staff abuse (p725).
- Resident-to-resident verbal, physical and sexual mistreatment also occurs; institutional safeguards do not eliminate the need for vigilance (p726).
