# Chapter 100 Study: Myopathies, Polymyalgia Rheumatica, and Giant Cell Arteritis

## Summary

**Myopathy assessment**
- Most myopathies cause proximal weakness; evaluate comorbidity, medications, functional impact and alternative metabolic/systemic causes (p1589–1591).
- Inclusion body myositis is the commonest inflammatory myopathy in older adults: slow, often asymmetric finger-flexor/quadriceps weakness, falls and possible dysphagia (p1589, p1595).
- Normal or only mildly elevated muscle enzymes do not exclude inclusion body myositis; biopsy and the characteristic clinical pattern matter (p1595–1596).
- Persistent weakness or CK elevation after statin withdrawal suggests another myopathy or immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy; anti-HMGCR antibodies may be relevant (p1598).
- For inflammatory myopathies, assess swallowing/lung involvement and malignancy; glucocorticoids with early steroid-sparing treatment require attention to infection, renal/hepatic disease and drug interactions (p1594–1595).

**Polymyalgia rheumatica**
- PMR produces symmetric neck/shoulder/hip-girdle pain and stiffness, often with constitutional symptoms; exclude myopathy, malignancy, infection and other rheumatic disease (p1601).
- GCA occurs in an estimated **15–20%** of PMR patients; conversely, PMR occurs in **40–60%** of GCA patients (p1600).
- Prednisone: minimum effective initial dose within **12.5–25 mg daily**. Improvement should appear by **2 weeks**, with near-complete response by **4 weeks** (p1601).
- Reduce toward **10 mg/day** within **4–8 weeks**, then slowly during maintained remission; the chapter describes decrements of **1 mg every 4 weeks** (p1601–1602).
- Poor initial response warrants reconsidering the diagnosis. Follow symptoms, not ESR alone, and monitor cumulative steroid toxicity (p1602).

**Giant cell arteritis**
- New headache, scalp sensitivity, jaw claudication and visual symptoms are key clues; constitutional illness or PMR can predominate (p1603).
- Negative temporal artery biopsy does not exclude GCA. Ideally obtain biopsy within **1 week** of starting steroids, but do not delay treatment for it (p1605).
- Suspected GCA: prednisone **1 mg/kg/day**, commonly **40–60 mg daily**; active visual symptoms warrant higher-dose urgent treatment as described in the full chapter (p1605).
- Treatment often lasts around **2 years**, with slow taper and relapse surveillance; inflammatory markers supplement rather than replace clinical assessment (p1605).

**Exam traps**
- PMR and GCA overlap but require different initial steroid doses; visual risk makes suspected GCA an immediate treatment priority (p1600–1605).
- Steroids can both treat inflammatory disease and cause myopathy; distinguish drug toxicity from persistent inflammatory weakness (p1589, p1594).
