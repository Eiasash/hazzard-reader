# Chapter 38 Study: Benign Prostate Disorders

## Summary

**Definitions**
- BPH is histologic hyperplasia; benign enlargement, bladder outlet obstruction and lower urinary tract symptoms overlap only partly (p555).
- LUTS include storage and voiding symptoms and can reflect comorbidity, medications or functional impairment rather than prostate disease alone (p555).

**Assessment**
- IPSS/AUASI scores **0–7** are mild, **8–19** moderate and **20–35** severe; symptom bother and quality of life also guide decisions (p559).
- Examine cognition, mobility, neurologic function, prostate/rectal findings and stool impaction; rectal examination is inaccurate for prostate volume (p561).
- Urinalysis is routine; PSA testing follows appropriate cancer-screening decisions rather than automatically accompanying LUTS (p561).
- Urodynamics is not routine initially. High bladder pressure with low flow supports obstruction; consider testing after inadequate response or before surgery in selected frail patients (p562).

**Management & drugs**
- Address reversible contributors and use shared decisions; many patients can begin with education, behavioral measures or watchful waiting (p563, p566).
- Tamsulosin **0.4–0.8 mg daily** or alfuzosin **10 mg daily** can improve LUTS; dizziness and hypotension still occur despite greater selectivity (p566).
- Tamsulosin can cause ejaculatory effects and floppy iris syndrome. Alert the cataract surgeon; stopping it does not reliably eliminate the risk (p566).
- Finasteride/dutasteride shrink the prostate and reduce progression risk, but symptom improvement can take **6–10 months**; sexual adverse effects are possible (p566–567).
- These reductase inhibitors lower PSA by **40–60%** during the first year, complicating interpretation where screening is appropriate (p567).
- Tadalafil **5 mg daily** is approved for male LUTS in the chapter; combining PDE inhibitors with alpha blockers can cause dangerous hypotension (p566–567).
- Add-on antimuscarinic or mirabegron benefit is modest; weigh antimuscarinic harms, blood-pressure effects and limited evidence in patients with large residuals (p567).

**Exam traps**
- Alpha blockers treat symptoms rapidly relative to reductase inhibitors, but the latter have evidence for reducing retention/surgery risk (p566–567).
- Prostate size, obstruction and symptom severity correlate weakly; a high symptom score does not itself diagnose obstruction (p555, p562).
