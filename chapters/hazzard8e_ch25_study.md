# Chapter 25 Study: Patient-Centered Management of Chronic Diseases

## Summary

**Self-management is part of clinical care**
- Patients manage most chronic illness at home; clinicians and systems must support knowledge, skills and practical capacity (p345-346).
- Core skills include problem solving, decisions, resource use, partnership, tailoring and action; tasks include medical, emotional and role management (p346).
- Support draws on behavioral models and organized chronic care: education alone is only one component (p347-350).
- Options include individual/group education, coaching, care management and technology; match the approach to patient needs and available support (p350-352).

**Match the model to the person**
- Single-disease guidelines fit functional adults resembling trial populations better than people with complex multimorbidity (p349-350).
- Symptom-focused and palliative approaches may fit advanced illness; intermediate complexity often requires explicit trade-offs among goals (p350).
- Disease-specific interventions can improve selected outcomes, but effects and outcome measures vary; evidence for multimorbidity support is less developed (p351-354).
- Housing, food, finances, access, trust and culture shape the ability to self-manage; community resources and tailored support matter (p354-355).
- Applying multiple guidelines can create drug–drug, drug–disease and disease–disease harms, alongside treatment burden (p355).
- The book describes **2 hours/day** of self-care plus another **2 hours/visit** spent in health facilities for patients with multiple chronic conditions and caregivers (p355).

**Care around priorities**
- Ask what outcomes matter and what treatments the patient is willing and able to undertake; independence and meaningful activities may outweigh disease targets (p355-356).
- Patient Priorities Care identifies values, actionable outcome goals, care preferences and the main issue to address (p356, p358).
- Use priorities to decide which treatments to start, continue or stop; serial trials can clarify benefit, burden and trade-offs (p356, p358).
- VA Whole Health combines the person, self-care, professional care and community; the approach can apply beyond veterans (p356, p358).
- Coordinate across clinicians and involve care partners. Revisit decisions as health, capacity and priorities change (p356-358).
