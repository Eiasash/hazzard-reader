# Chapter 24 Study: Integrative Medicine and Health

## Summary

**Whole-person assessment**
- Integrative care combines conventional and complementary approaches; ask directly about use, stress, beliefs, support and supplements (p329-331).
- Weigh benefits against polypharmacy, product variability and drug–supplement interactions; disclosure is often incomplete (p331-332).

**Supplements: evidence and cautions**
- Vitamin D has bone benefit in institutionalized older adults; cognitive and other benefits remain uncertain. Magnesium evidence varies by indication (p332).
- Fish oil: **1000–2000 mg/day** EPA/DHA is discussed for potential cardiovascular benefit, but trials conflict; no convincing Alzheimer-treatment benefit (p332, p334).
- Coenzyme Q10: **100 mg three times daily** improved outcomes in one moderate–severe reduced-EF heart-failure trial as an adjunct to standard treatment; well tolerated (p334).
- Ginkgo: **240 mg/day** standardized extract may help selected cognitive symptoms; evidence conflicts. Use cautiously with bleeding disorders or anticoagulants (p334-335).
- Vitamin E does not prevent MCI progression; some high-dose meta-analyses suggest increased mortality (p335).
- Glucosamine/chondroitin: generally not recommended for osteoarthritis in the cited guidance; hand-OA chondroitin is a conditional exception, **800 mg/day** in one trial (p335).
- SAM-e: **200–1600 mg/day** in divided doses depending on indication/route; empty-stomach absorption and possible mania matter (p335-336).
- Melatonin: start **0.3–2 mg** immediate release **1 hour** before bed; long-term adverse-effect evidence is limited (p336).
- Valerian: **300–900 mg** standardized extract before bed; effects may take weeks, studies conflict, and severe liver disease warrants caution (p336-337).
- Black cohosh: studied Remifemin **20 mg once or twice daily**; heterogeneous evidence, uncertain long-term safety and caution when estrogen is contraindicated (p337).
- Saw palmetto: **160 mg twice daily**; formulation-dependent, conflicting results, usually mild adverse effects (p337).

**Movement and manual approaches**
- Tai chi reduces falls in meta-analyses; yoga and meditation may help selected physical/psychological outcomes, with variable evidence (p338-340).
- Acupuncture has its strongest evidence for chronic pain; chiropractic evidence in older adults is more limited, and cervical manipulation has rare serious risks (p340-341).
- Ayurveda and Traditional Chinese Medicine combine multiple approaches; align any chosen modality with the person's goals and conventional care (p342).
