# Chapter 23 Study: Substance Use and Disorders

## Summary

**Identify unhealthy use**
- Unhealthy use includes risk of harm or established health/social consequences; addiction is a health condition, not a moral failure (p317, p321).
- Aging, multimorbidity and interacting medicines increase harm, sometimes at lower exposure than in younger adults (p320–321).
- A U.S. standard drink contains **14 g** ethanol. The chapter cites daily limits of two drinks for men and one for women on drinking days; contraindicating conditions/medications may require abstinence (p317).
- Reduced tolerance and changed social roles can obscure substance-use diagnostic criteria in older adults (p322).

**Screen & assess**
- Ask nonjudgmentally about alcohol, prescriptions, nonprescription drugs, cannabis and other substances; recommended screening includes older adults (p321–322).
- Use AUDIT/AUDIT-C or geriatric tools such as MAST-G and CARET; consider comorbidity and drug interactions, not consumption alone (p322–323).
- Positive screens require assessment of use pattern, consequences, severity, supports and treatment needs (p323).

**Treatment**
- Brief interventions, motivational approaches and psychosocial treatment can help; tailor goals and supports to the person (p323–324).
- Alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal may need closely monitored specialty care (p324).
- Naltrexone: oral daily or monthly injection; contraindicated with prescribed opioids, acute hepatitis or liver failure (p324).
- Acamprosate: oral three times daily; reduce for renal insufficiency. Evidence is strongest after initial abstinence in younger-adult trials (p324).
- Disulfiram: generally not recommended in older adults because the alcohol reaction can be harmful; if used, great caution is required (p324).
- Opioid-use disorder has medication options including methadone, buprenorphine and naltrexone; combine medication access with supportive care (p324–326).
- The chapter has limited older-adult trial evidence for many medications; use its source dose table and individual precautions (p324–325).
- Continue follow-up and reassessment; recurrence can occur in the course of chronic substance-use disorders (p321).
