# Chapter 12 Study: Age-Friendly Care

## Summary

**Use the framework as a set**
- Age-friendly care integrates **4Ms**: What Matters, Medication, Mentation and Mobility across care settings (p172).
- Apply them together to shift attention toward function, wellness and the person's goals; isolated disease plans can conflict (p172).
- High-functioning teams need shared purpose, clear roles, communication, mutual respect and recognition of each member's expertise (p171-172).
- Include older adults, families and caregivers as partners; the approach aims to improve experience, population health and cost of care (p172).

**Translate each element into care**
- What Matters: elicit and document specific health-outcome goals and care preferences, including end-of-life care, and use them consistently across providers (p172-173).
- Adapt conversations to preferred language, culture, hearing and literacy; do not assume family roles or personal values (p174).
- Medication: prescribe when needed, review affordability and understanding, and avoid interference with mentation, mobility or patient priorities (p174-175).
- Consider deprescribing or dose adjustment when clinically appropriate; ask about withdrawal fears and other barriers, involving patients and caregivers (p174).
- Computerized support and STOPP/START can identify prescribing problems, but assessment and communication remain team responsibilities (p174-175).
- Mentation: prevent, identify and manage delirium, dementia and depression. Dementia is not normal aging (p175).
- Person-centered dementia care recognizes the individual, accepts the person's reality, supports meaningful engagement and relationships, and reassesses care (p175).
- Mobility: enable safe daily movement, strength and balance; address chronic-disease effects and use tailored physical-activity support (p176).

**Measure what changes**
- Integrate the framework into routine workflows and communicate across settings; use patient goals and functional outcomes to guide improvement (p172-177).
- The book's implementation examples report reduced utilization and cost in some systems; these are local results, not guaranteed effects everywhere (p176-177).
- Annual wellness visits can provide an opportunity to review the framework and engage families in a coordinated plan (p177).
