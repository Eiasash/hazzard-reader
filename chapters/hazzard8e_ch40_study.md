# Chapter 40 Study: Applied Clinical Geroscience

## Summary

**The geroscience hypothesis**
- Shared biological aging mechanisms may drive multiple chronic diseases and geriatric syndromes; targeting them could extend healthspan (p593-595).
- Hallmarks interact: altered metabolism, macromolecular damage, epigenetics, inflammation, stress responses, proteostasis, stem-cell changes and senescence (p594).
- Geriatric syndromes share functional risk factors and multifactorial causes. No single molecule explains aging (p595-597).
- An upstream target may influence several downstream processes; pathway plausibility alone does not prove clinical benefit (p597, p602-603).

**Evidence and trial design**
- Rapamycin, metformin and senolytics are investigational geroscience approaches; much efficacy evidence remains preclinical (p597-599).
- Dasatinib plus quercetin has early human pilot evidence in specific conditions; this does not establish routine treatment for aging (p599).
- CALERIE studied **25% calorie restriction** in nonobese adults, reporting improvements in disease-risk measures and aging biomarkers (p599).
- Healthspan trials target gradual accumulation of disease and disability; resilience trials target recovery after stressors such as surgery or myocardial infarction (p599-600).
- Measure clinically meaningful function, independence, disease accumulation and survival; biomarkers may be secondary outcomes (p600).
- Include heterogeneous older adults, chronic conditions and care settings. Limited life expectancy and severe impairment require careful consideration of the chance to benefit within the study period (p600-601).

**Biomarkers and counseling**
- No single clinical-trial endpoint or aging biomarker is universally accepted; a marker should relate to outcomes and respond meaningfully to intervention (p601-602).
- Multisystem indices and biomarker panels may be more informative than a single pathway; telomere measures have substantial variability (p602).
- DNA-methylation clocks estimate aging-related patterns; evidence that interventions change them remains an evolving research area (p602).
- Unregulated clinics are not controlled trials. Most investigational therapies fail, and older adults may be especially vulnerable to harms (p602-603).
- Some age-related changes may be adaptive or consequences of aging rather than causes. Clinical geroscience needs combined biological, geriatric and trial expertise (p603).
