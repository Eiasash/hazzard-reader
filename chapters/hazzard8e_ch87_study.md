# Chapter 87 Study: Constipation

## Summary

### Definition & Epidemiology
- Rome IV: ≥2 of the following, on >**25%** of occasions, for ≥**12 wk** in the last **12 mo**: ≤**2 BM/week**, straining, hard stool (Bristol 1-2), sensation of incomplete evacuation, sensation of anorectal obstruction, manual maneuvers to defecate (p1354).
- North American prevalence **12-19%**; an estimated **63 million** people in North America meet Rome II criteria for constipation (p1354).
- Self-reported constipation in adults 65+: **26%** women, **16%** men; rises to **34%/26%** in the 84+ group (p1354).
- Normal aging does NOT reduce bowel movement frequency: only **1-7%** of any age group report ≤2 BM/wk. Among older adults who self-report "constipation," **<10%** actually have ≤2 BM/wk and **>50%** move bowels daily — straining (**65%**) and hard stool (**39%**) drive the complaint, not frequency; **21%** have rectal outlet delay (p1355).
- New-onset constipation incidence in nursing homes screened every 3 months: **7%** (p1355).
- LTC prevalence is far higher than community: a Finnish study found chronic constipation/rectal outlet delay in **79%/81%** (women/men) of nursing home residents; **50-74%** of LTC residents use daily laxatives (p1355).

### Pathophysiology
- Normal total gut transit <**72 h**; older adults with chronic constipation average **4-9 days**, extending to **6 days to >14 days** if bedridden/institutionalized (p1356).
- Slowed transit reduces stool water content (normally **75%**), shrinking fecal bulk and further blunting propulsive activity — a self-worsening cycle (p1356).
- **37%** of non-constipated older adults cannot expel a simulated stool sphere on evacuation testing — some "abnormal" anorectal test findings are normal-aging, not disease (p1357).
- Table 87-4 age-related mechanisms of chronic constipation (owner-highlighted): increased colonic collagen deposition, reduced inhibitory nerve input to circular muscle, and increased plasma-endorphin binding to gut receptors (p1356).

### Risk Factors
- Polypharmacy (≥**5 medications**) is an established risk factor (p1359); nursing home residents average **6-9 meds/day** (p1358). Anticholinergics are the strongest single drug class implicated.
- Oral iron >**325 mg/day**: no further increase in absorption, just more GI side effects — cap the dose (p1359).
- Calcium supplementation has a real but modest effect: **13%** vs **9.1%** placebo over a 5-year trial (p1359).
- Parkinson disease: **59%** meet Rome criteria (vs **21%** in age-matched controls); pelvic dyssynergia affects **60%** of PD patients (p1360).
- Stroke: **60%** of patients are constipated during inpatient rehab (p1360).
- Diabetes: colonic transit prolongs to **200 ± 144 h** in immobile diabetics. If using metoclopramide, limit to <**12 weeks** continuous use with a **2-week** drug holiday (tardive dyskinesia risk) (p1361).
- <3 BM/week carries a >**2-fold** colon cancer risk in adjusted studies — constipation itself is not an indication for colonoscopy, but a genuine change in bowel habit should prompt workup (p1361).

### Complications
- Overflow fecal incontinence in frail nursing home residents: enemas-until-clear then lactulose achieved **94%** resolution — yet only **4%** of these residents are referred for further assessment (p1361).
- Fecal impaction was the primary reason for **27%** of admissions in one UK acute geriatric unit survey (p1362).
- Constipation → urinary retention: PVR >**100 mL** carries a **4-fold** risk increase; treating the constipation dropped mean PVR from **85→30 mL** over 4 months (p1362).
- Acute colonic pseudo-obstruction (Ogilvie syndrome): cecal diameter ≥**10 cm** on x-ray (p1362).
- Diverticular disease affects **30-60%** of adults over 60 (p1363).

### Evaluation
- Digital rectal exam is required in everyone reporting constipation; an empty rectum on exam does NOT exclude the diagnosis (p1364).
- Normal perineal descent is <**4 cm** (p1365). Plain x-ray: megacolon = colon diameter >**6.5 cm**; rectal dilatation >**4 cm** implies dysmotility (p1365).
- Colonic transit marker study: normal clears to ≤**5 of 24** markers by day **5**; balloon expulsion normal range **1-5 min** (p1368).
- Colonoscopy is NOT indicated for constipation alone — cancer detection was only **2%** for constipation vs **12%** for rectal bleeding in one octogenarian series (p1368).

### Nonpharmacologic Management
- Fiber target **20-35 g/day**; start low at **3-4 g/day** and titrate up to limit bloating (p1370).
- Fluid **1.5-2 L/day** significantly potentiates the effect of added fiber (p1370).
- Biofeedback outperforms laxatives/diazepam for rectal outlet delay/dyssynergia: **70% vs 23%** response at 3 months, **80% vs 22%** at 6-12 months (p1371).

### Drugs (name — dose — key cautions)
- **Senna** — 1-3 tabs at bedtime; onset **8-12 h** (p1372); cheap, safe first-line stimulant.
- **Bisacodyl** — **10 mg** PO daily (p1375); caution: bisacodyl used for colonoscopy prep may raise ischemic colitis risk in patients with cardiovascular disease (p1368).
- **Psyllium/bulk agents** — 1-3×/day with fluids; adds **1.4** BM/wk vs placebo (p1375); caution: impaction risk if fluid intake is poor.
- **Lactulose/sorbitol** — 15-30 mL/day; adds **1.9** BM/wk (p1376); sorbitol equally effective and cheaper.
- **PEG** — most potent osmotic; disimpaction regimen **1 L (8 sachets)/day × 3 days** achieved **89%** success (p1376); caution: liquid stools.
- **Magnesium salts** — avoid magnesium hydroxide in CKD; magnesium citrate not recommended in older adults at all (pseudo-obstruction risk) (p1376).
- **Docusate** — avoid: no true laxative effect even at **300 mg/day**, despite being the most commonly prescribed nursing-home agent (p1376).
- **Glycerin suppository** — first-line for rectal outlet delay; **bisacodyl suppository** second-line; onset **5-45 min** (p1377).
- **Lubiprostone 24 mcg BID**; **linaclotide**; **plecanatide 3 or 6 mg** — secretagogues; effective but costly, with limited older-adult trial data (p1377).
- **Prucalopride** — 5-HT4 agonist; unlike older 5-HT4 agents (cisapride, tegaserod), no significant cardiac-arrhythmia signal (p1378).
- **PAMORAs for opioid-induced constipation** — naldemedine (**52% vs 35%** achieving >3 CSBM/wk vs placebo) and naloxegol (**42% vs 29%**) (p1378).
- Table 87-9 laxative mechanisms (owner-highlighted): bisacodyl/picosulfate are prodrugs converted by colonic bacteria/brush-border enzymes to an antiabsorptive, prokinetic metabolite; senna/anthraquinones alter electrolyte transport and stimulate the myenteric plexus to increase motility (p1373–1374).

## Drill

**1. What Rome IV threshold and time frame define chronic constipation?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

≥2 of: ≤2 BM/wk, straining, hard stool (Bristol 1-2), incomplete evacuation, anorectal obstruction sensation, manual maneuvers — on >25% of occasions for ≥12 wk in the last 12 mo (p1354).

</details>

**2. Roughly what proportion of older North Americans meet formal criteria for constipation?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

12-19% prevalence; about 63 million people in North America meet Rome II criteria (p1354).

</details>

**3. Does normal aging itself reduce bowel movement frequency?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No — weekly BM frequency is essentially unchanged with age; only 1-7% of any age group report ≤2 BM/wk (p1355).

</details>

**4. What incidence of new-onset constipation was found in nursing home residents screened every 3 months?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

7% (p1355).

</details>

**5. How prolonged is total gut transit time in bedridden/institutionalized older adults with constipation, versus normal?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

6 days to more than 14 days, versus a normal total transit of under 72 hours (p1356).

</details>

**6. What fraction of non-constipated older adults fail to expel a test sphere on simulated defecation testing?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

37% — a reminder some anorectal test "abnormalities" reflect normal aging (p1357).

</details>

**7. Above what daily oral iron dose does absorption plateau while GI side effects increase?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

325 mg/day (p1359).

</details>

**8. What percentage of Parkinson disease patients meet Rome criteria for constipation, versus age-matched controls?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

59% vs 21% in controls (p1360).

</details>

**9. Why must metoclopramide use in older adults be time-limited, and for how long is it safe continuously?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Risk of tardive dyskinesia rises with continuous use beyond 12 weeks; use a 2-week drug holiday (p1361).

</details>

**10. What treatment regimen resolved overflow fecal incontinence in 94% of frail nursing home residents?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Daily enemas until no further response, followed by lactulose (p1361).

</details>

**11. What PVR cutoff and relative risk link constipation to urinary retention in hospitalized older women?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

PVR >100 mL; constipation increased retention risk 4-fold (p1362).

</details>

**12. What cecal diameter on plain x-ray defines Ogilvie syndrome (acute colonic pseudo-obstruction)?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

≥10 cm (p1362).

</details>

**13. Is colonoscopy indicated for constipation alone in older adults?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No — cancer yield for constipation as the sole indication was only 2% (vs 12% for rectal bleeding) in one series; workup is warranted only with systemic features, bleeding, or unexplained change in habit (p1368).

</details>

**14. What are the recommended daily fiber and fluid targets for treating constipation?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Fiber 20-35 g/day (start at 3-4 g/day and titrate); fluid 1.5-2 L/day (p1370).

</details>

**15. Why should docusate be avoided as a first-line laxative despite being the most-prescribed nursing-home agent?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It has little or no true laxative effect, even at doses of 300 mg/day (p1376).

</details>
