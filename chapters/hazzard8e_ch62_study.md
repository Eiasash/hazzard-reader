# Chapter 62 Study: Cerebrovascular Disease

## Summary

**Mechanism & diagnosis**
- Determine ischemic mechanism: large-artery, lacunar, cardioembolic, cryptogenic or other. Etiology guides treatment and prevention (p954).
- Obtain urgent brain and vascular imaging. Noncontrast CT rapidly excludes hemorrhage; a negative early CT does not exclude infarction (p953–954).
- MRI diffusion imaging is more sensitive for acute infarction; CTA/MRA identifies major arterial disease and large-vessel occlusion (p953, p965).

**Acute ischemic treatment**
- In eligible patients, alteplase **0.9 mg/kg**, with **10%** as a bolus and the rest over **1 hour**, reduces disability; review hemorrhage risk and exclusions (p966).
- Earlier treatment is better. The chapter supports the selected **3–4.5-hour** window and imaging-based selection for some wake-up/extended-window presentations (p967).
- Thrombectomy benefits selected disabling large-vessel occlusions; imaging-selected DAWN patients were **6–24 hours** from last-known-well. Favorable tissue imaging is not a reason to delay treatment (p968–969).
- After IV thrombolysis, maintain BP below **180/105 mm Hg** for at least **24 hours** and obtain follow-up CT/MRI; antithrombotics are generally withheld until hemorrhage is excluded (p969).

**Secondary prevention**
- Aspirin **50–325 mg/day** is commonly used after ischemic stroke/TIA when appropriate; consider bleeding history and the post-thrombolysis restriction (p970–971).
- Match antithrombotic treatment to etiology; routine anticoagulation for embolic stroke of undetermined source did not improve outcomes and increased bleeding in the trials summarized (p974).
- Long-term aspirin/clopidogrel combination after lacunar stroke increased bleeding and death without superior stroke prevention (p973).
- Control vascular risks; the Treat Stroke to Target trial supports LDL below **70 mg/dL** after recent stroke/TIA with atherosclerotic disease (p975).

**Hemorrhage & exam traps**
- Deep hemorrhage commonly reflects hypertension; lobar microbleeds suggest cerebral amyloid angiopathy. Imaging, not symptoms alone, distinguishes hemorrhage from infarction (p975–976).
- Suspected subarachnoid hemorrhage starts with CT; persistent suspicion after negative CT requires lumbar puncture (p978).
- Oral nimodipine **60 mg every 4 hours** after SAH reduces delayed ischemia and mortality in the cited trials (p978).
- Age alone does not negate reperfusion benefit; disabling deficits and eligibility matter. Incorporate rehabilitation, symptom control and palliative goals throughout care (p968–969, p978).
