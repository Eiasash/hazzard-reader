# Chapter 36 Study: Gynecologic Disorders

## Summary

**Assessment & vulvovaginal symptoms**
- Ask about bleeding and pelvic/vulvovaginal pain even when a visit addresses other concerns; review surgical, hormonal and screening history (p527–528).
- Pelvic pain often arises outside reproductive organs; assess urinary, gastrointestinal and musculoskeletal causes (p527, p530).
- Persistent or atypical vulvar lesions merit examination and possible biopsy; lichen sclerosus can scar and has malignant potential (p531–532).
- Discharge requires examination: candidiasis, bacterial vaginosis, sexually transmitted infection and atrophy have different findings (p534).
- Genitourinary syndrome of menopause may cause dryness, dyspareunia and urinary symptoms; low-dose local estrogen is a treatment option (p534–536).

**Bleeding & cancer**
- Postmenopausal bleeding requires endometrial assessment, even if other findings are present (p539).
- Sampling may be deferred at an endometrial thickness of **4 mm or less**, but biopsy is preferable in older women or recurrent/persistent bleeding regardless of imaging (p539).
- Stopping cervical screening depends on adequate previous negative screening and low risk, not age alone (p536–537).
- Ultrasound/CA-125 screening is ineffective in low-risk women; investigate suspicious persistent symptoms (p527, p539).
- Most adnexal masses are benign after menopause; transvaginal ultrasound helps characterize them (p539).

**Hormones & pelvic support**
- Systemic hormone decisions require individualized assessment of symptoms and vascular/cancer risks; the book distinguishes continuing therapy from late initiation (p540–541).
- Prolapse treatment depends on bother, overall health and goals. Observation may suit uncomplicated, minimally bothersome prolapse (p544).
- Refer for nonhealing ulceration, bleeding, retention causing recurrent infection, or hydronephrosis (p544).
- Pelvic-floor exercises and pessaries offer conservative options; assess tissue health and ability to manage the device (p544–545).
- Less mobile or cognitively impaired pessary users may need close follow-up within **1–3 months**; tailor the interval to the patient (p545).
