# Chapter 66 Study: General Topics in Geriatric Psychiatry

## Summary

**Assessment & psychosis**
- Assess medical illness, medications, cognition, mood, substance use and social context; brief cognitive screens differ in scope and do not replace evaluation (p1024–1026).
- New psychosis can accompany delirium, mood or neurocognitive disorders, as well as a primary psychotic illness (p1026).
- Antipsychotics require individualized risk–benefit discussion and monitoring; older adults are vulnerable to adverse effects, and dementia-related use has mortality concerns (p1027–1028).

**Mood & suicide**
- Depression is common and associated with suicide risk; late-life mood presentations require medical and psychiatric assessment (p1028–1029).
- Ask directly about suicide, intent, means and protective factors while establishing rapport; older patients may be reluctant to disclose thoughts (p1029–1030).
- High acute suicide risk requires emergent mental-health evaluation. Reduce access to lethal means, treat the underlying disorder and involve support people (p1030–1031).

**Anxiety & sleep**
- Distinguish primary anxiety from medical illness, medication effects, intoxication or withdrawal; symptom timing matters (p1032).
- Psychotherapy and SSRIs/SNRIs are central treatments. Geriatric starting doses are generally half those for younger adults (p1034).
- The book describes maximal SSRI antianxiety effect after **6–12 months** at a therapeutic dose; buspirone may take **2–4 weeks** (p1034).
- Benzodiazepines warrant great caution: cognitive effects, dependence and falls. TCAs add anticholinergic/cardiac risks (p1034).
- Sleep complaints are often multifactorial; review pain, nocturia, breathing, drugs and substances before attributing them to aging (p1034–1035).
- CBT for insomnia has better long-term outcomes than medication; CPAP treats sleep-disordered breathing (p1035–1036).

**Substances, personality & adaptation**
- Alcohol and benzodiazepine misuse can worsen cognition, falls and mental illness; withdrawal may be hazardous (p1037–1038).
- Distinguish longstanding personality patterns from active illness; treat comorbid disorders and target functional problems (p1039–1040).
- Successful aging can coexist with illness: support adaptation, resilience, physical/cognitive activity and social connection (p1040–1041).
