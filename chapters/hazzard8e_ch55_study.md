## Selecting an aid by the task

<span data-exam-page="828"></span>

A **pick-up walker** provides bilateral support on a stationary frame. The patient can unload a leg completely, which explains the choice before a unilateral amputee has a prosthesis. Lifting the frame for each step costs strength and coordination and slows gait (p828, Table 55-5).

A **two-wheeled walker** advances more easily and permits a smoother gait. Its fixed wheels make turns less smooth than a rollator's, but two wheels give more stability than four. The table lists Parkinson disease as a situation in which it may help. June 2023 described freezing and backward falls (p827–828).

A **four-wheeled rollator** turns easily, negotiates outdoor surfaces and provides a seat. For a patient limited by exertional dyspnea, the chance to sit and recover matters. The price of that mobility is less stability and the need to coordinate the hand brakes before sitting or standing (p827–828).

<span data-practice-ids="mcq-9e41920180218015f9a52e2c mcq-0a63b1fdce0707c8e2c49e60 mcq-f36a867b1cc82a728769c382" data-reader-metadata="exam"></span>

<span data-exam-page="827"></span>

A **straight cane** widens the base of support and provides sensory feedback through the hand. Advance the cane with the involved leg, holding it in the opposite hand. The June 2026 patient had left hip abductor weakness and needed minimal help with balance, making a single point in the right hand appropriate. A quad cane accepts more weight and can help in hemiparesis, but its heavier, wider base may catch the foot or fit poorly on a stair (p827–828).

**Stairs reverse the sequence.** Ascend first with the uninvolved leg while the cane and involved leg support from below. Descend first with the cane and involved leg, keeping the stronger leg above to control lowering. September 2024 asked which leg descends first after a right ankle fracture (p827).

## Hip surgery, precautions and mobilization

<span data-exam-page="832"></span>

Early mobilization after hip fracture limits deconditioning and the complications of bed rest. Fracture stability determines permissible loading after repair. When feasible, weight bearing as tolerated is easier for older adults to achieve than touchdown loading. June 2023 asked about mobility on the first postoperative day after uncomplicated hip replacement. Its official answer permits loading as tolerated, while the passage that explicitly contrasts loading regimens describes fracture repair (p832).

After a **traditional posterolateral hip replacement**, crossing the legs, excessive flexion and rotating the leg may increase the risk of early dislocation. A raised toilet seat reduces the flexion required to sit down. May 2024 tested crossing the legs. Knee replacement calls for a different early task: controlling pain and swelling so that movement and quadriceps work can restore range (p832).

## Cardiac rehabilitation and medical stability

<span data-exam-page="830"></span>

The patient in June 2023 had reduced ejection fraction but no active congestion. Exercise can address peripheral muscle conditioning as well as cardiovascular adaptations, reducing exercise intolerance and improving daily function. Hazzard calls for controlled fluid status and clinical stability for at least three to four weeks before starting exercise in heart failure. A remote admission alone does not establish readiness (p830).

A cardiac rehabilitation program also provides monitoring, risk-factor management and support for adherence. In the Medicare cohort cited by Hazzard, participants had lower mortality than nonparticipants. That observation describes outcomes in the cohort and does not quantify an individual patient's expected benefit (p830).

## Measuring function and choosing a team intervention

<span data-exam-page="820"></span>

**Five timed rises from a chair** test lower-extremity strength. Table 55-1 links that attribute to walking and stair climbing, which is why June 2026 selected it. Gait speed measures dynamic performance, progressively narrower standing positions challenge static balance, and life-space records where the person actually moved in the preceding month (p820–821).

<span data-exam-page="825"></span>

**Occupational therapy** turns improvement into something the patient can do, such as eating, dressing or grooming. June 2025 asked about a patient already destined for nursing care. The destination changes how training is planned, but self-care still matters. The official reference list prints p825. The explicit list of self-care evaluation, training, assistive technology and home assessment is in Table 55-4 on p823.

## Stroke recovery and readiness to participate

<span data-practice-ids="mcq-610da062dae0a19d869b2ef7 mcq-bcae0dab35a2e9279066d221 mcq-ef77c3f440fa29a46ca59282 mcq-93ed9b58f5e9451decd9ae85" data-reader-metadata="exam"></span>

<span data-exam-ids="mcq-de35ec8f8613614fad033467 mcq-5323c51583402673a5d61738"></span>

September 2024 Q36 asked about apraxia: difficulty executing a learned action despite understanding the instruction and recovering strength. June 2026 Q92 asked for a different inference: transfers and communication improved while walking and stairs stayed at the lowest FIM score. The accepted explanation is a continuing weight-bearing restriction. The chart measures performance and does not identify the fracture. Official locators: September 2024 reference list, Q36, Stroke Rehabilitation Clinician Handbook p15; June 2026 album, Figure 4.

After stroke, inability to swallow safely and inability to communicate are separate barriers to rehabilitation. Aspiration can be silent in up to one-third of **patients with dysphagia**. Absence of coughing therefore cannot establish safe swallowing. The 2020 paper tested the denominator, alongside rates of incontinence, depression and shoulder pain (p831).

When tone and motor control change in the hemiplegic shoulder, positioning protects the joint from subluxation and early range-of-motion work helps prevent contracture. Those are separate aims of the treatment in 2020 Q50. The page supports positioning without naming a particular sling (p831).

Cognitive impairment calls for an assessment of ability to learn and follow instructions. A reversible acute problem such as delirium can delay that assessment. June 2022 Q33 contrasts a patient who can follow simple commands with patients whose current illness or participation limits effective treatment. Establish what the patient could do before the event and reassess after stabilization (p820–821).

## Israeli context — שיקום גריאטרי בישראל

<span data-practice-ids="mcq-f7cdd68518d6671b51791c12 mcq-e9726c2e2313e5408517930a mcq-584efd4ae05bbe5c48d0409f mcq-6ba05d11f3ef3af2f94b0786 mcq-fda71abd73e488940f33d8a8" data-reader-metadata="exam"></span>

<span data-exam-ids="mcq-f6c1d45922e1027bb5ab4324 mcq-92187e5ab10d4fe7ecd14771 mcq-b68a3e95e9873facb2f5f6cc mcq-1bbe7663ed422d20fc60e429"></span>

<div lang="he" dir="rtl">
<p><strong>מי מתאים לשיקום?</strong> ההערכה משלבת את המצב הרפואי הנוכחי, התפקוד לפני האירוע, יכולת הלמידה ושיתוף הפעולה. לאחר מחלה חריפה שמונעת מאמץ שיקומי יש להעריך מחדש. ירידה קוגניטיבית קודמת אינה שוללת שיקום אם המטופל מסוגל להשתתף ולהפיק תועלת. פצע לחץ או הזנה בזונדה שאינם מפריעים לטיפול, וכן איסור דריכה זמני, אינם כשלעצמם סיבה לדחיית ההפניה. חוזר מינהל רפואה <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mr04-2009/he/files_circulars_mr_mr04_2009.pdf#page=4">4/2009</a>, סעיפים 5.2–5.4, עמ׳ 4.</p>
<p><strong>מי מחליט ומי אחראי?</strong> מומחה בגריאטריה או בשיקום בבית החולים מתייעץ עם הצוות הרב־מקצועי, משתף את המטופל ומשפחתו ומתעד את הנימוקים. האחריות למתן השיקום חלה על קופת החולים. ההמלצה מועברת לפחות שני ימי עבודה לפני השחרור. מומחה מטעם הקופה משיב בתוך שני ימי עבודה לכל היותר. סטייה מההמלצה מחייבת הנמקה ותוכנית חלופית. במחלוקת על הצורך בשיקום אפשר להפנות להערכה באשפוז שיקומי או לקבל חוות דעת נוספת ממומחה מוסכם. חוזר <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mr04-2009/he/files_circulars_mr_mr04_2009.pdf#page=5">4/2009</a>, סעיפים 4.1, 6.3–6.8, עמ׳ 3, 5–6.</p>
<p><strong>אשפוז, שיקום יום או בית.</strong> צורך בהשגחה רפואית וסיעודית ממושכת, בית שאינו מאפשר טיפול, ליקוי שדורש משאבים ייעודיים או היעדר מסגרת קהילתית מתאימה מצדיקים שיקום באשפוז. בקהילה ניתן לקבל טיפול רב־מקצועי בשיקום יום, טיפול ממקצוע אחד במכון או טיפול בבית. לאחר שחרור לקהילה נדרשת הערכה בתוך שני ימי עבודה מהשחרור והתאמת תוכנית ומסגרת בתוך חמישה ימי עבודה מההפניה. חוזר <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mr04-2009/he/files_circulars_mr_mr04_2009.pdf#page=6">4/2009</a>, סעיפים 7, 10.1–10.3, עמ׳ 6–8.</p>
<p><strong>משך ותוצאה.</strong> בשיקום אורתופדי באשפוז החוזר מציין לפחות 14 יום, ובשיקום נוירולוגי לפחות 25 יום. תקופה קצרה יותר טעונה נימוק ברשומה ובמכתב השחרור על סמך הערכה תפקודית כוללנית. יש להעריך בקבלה ולפני השחרור בכלים שמאפשרים להשוות תפקוד לאורך השיקום. לאחר אירוע מוחי או פגיעה נוירולוגית אחרת, סעיף 7.6 מונה ציון FIM נמוך מ־75 או Barthel נמוך מ־16 כהתוויה לשיקום באשפוז. אלה ספים לבחירת מסגרת באוכלוסייה המוגדרת. חוזר <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mr04-2009/he/files_circulars_mr_mr04_2009.pdf#page=6">4/2009</a>, סעיפים 7.6, 8.3–8.4, 9.2, 13.2, עמ׳ 6–7, 9.</p>
<p><strong>שיקום, סיעוד מורכב ואשפוז סיעודי בקוד.</strong> בבדיקת מועמד לאשפוז סיעודי מסובסד האחות בודקת אם מדובר בסיעוד מורכב ואם מוצו אפשרויות השיקום. ועדת הסיווג בראשות הגריאטר המחוזי קובעת התאמה לסיווג סיעודי או תשוש נפש. קבלה בקוד דורשת הפניה מאושרת מלשכת הבריאות, גם במעבר פנימי מסיעוד מורכב לאשפוז במימון המשרד. מקורות: חוזר מינהל רפואה <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mr20-2009/he/files_circulars_mr_mr20_2009.pdf">20/2009</a>, נספח נוהל 01-09, סעיפים 4.1, 9.2.1; נוהל <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/ng-3-4-2/he/files_circulars_ng_3_4_2.pdf">3.4.2</a>, סעיפים 3.1, 5.1–5.2.</p>
<p><strong>מי משלם באשפוז סיעודי כרוני?</strong> במסלול הקוד משרד הבריאות משלם למוסד וגובה השתתפות עצמית לפי הכללים ובהתאם ליכולת הכלכלית. חוזר מנכ״ל <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mk08-2018/he/files_circulars_mk_mk08_2018.pdf">8/2018</a> מיום 21.03.2018 החליף את חוזר 24/2005, שאליו מפנה נוהל 20/2009. התחולה המפורטת בחוזר החדש היא מ־08.04.2018. מקור: חוזר מנכ״ל 8/2018, עמ׳ 1–2, סעיפים 1–3, 6.</p>
<p><strong>מה ממשיך במחלקה סיעודית?</strong> הפיזיותרפיסט מתאים אביזרי ניידות וישיבה לצורכי הדייר, מדריך את הדייר והצוות בשימוש, ועוקב אחרי היעילות. אם נדרשת הערכת בליעה, קלינאי התקשורת מתעד את הממצאים ומדריך את המטפלים לגבי מרקמי מזון ושתייה ואכילה בטוחה. אלו הנחיות לעבודה במחלקות סיעודיות ותשושי נפש. נהלים <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/grc-1-6-0-1/he/files_circulars_ng_ngc_1_6_0_1.pdf">1.6.0.1</a> סעיף 4.4.3, <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/grc-1-6-0-3/he/files_circulars_ng_ngc_1_6_0_3.pdf">1.6.0.3</a> סעיף 5, ו־<a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/grc-1-9-0-1/he/files_circulars_ng_ngc_1_9_0_1.pdf">1.9.0.1</a> סעיפים 5.2.6–5.2.8, מיום 18.12.2025.</p>
<p><strong>מה תפקיד הביטוח הלאומי?</strong> בנוהל האשפוז המסובסד, הביטוח הלאומי קובע את הזכאות לסיוע מכוח חוק ביטוח בריאות ממלכתי כתנאי מקדמי לאשפוז סיעודי באמצעות משרד הבריאות. בתקופת ההמתנה העובדת הסוציאלית מדריכה את המשפחה לגבי עזרה בקהילה, ובכלל זה גמלת סיעוד מהביטוח הלאומי. ההמלצה לטיפול שיקומי נבחנת במסלול הרפואי של בית החולים וקופת החולים שתואר לעיל. חוזר <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mr20-2009/he/files_circulars_mr_mr20_2009.pdf">20/2009</a>, נספח נוהל 01-09, סעיפים 4.2, 14.2.1, עמ׳ 2, 9 בנספח.</p>

**Day rehabilitation in practice.** The Kiryat Bialik center initially accepted patients who needed at least two rehabilitation disciplines. Its aim was to shorten or replace hospitalization for suitable patients, mainly people recovering from stroke or living with other neurological disorders. This was the center's admission policy during the 2009–2011 evaluation. Brookdale, [RR-622-12 (2012)](https://brookdale.jdc.org.il/en/publication/multidisciplinary-rehabilitation-daycare-center-community-kiryat-bialik-evaluation-study/), publication summary, paragraphs 1–4.

**Access and outcome are different measures.** In the Israeli 2009–2010 hospital survey, 56.5% of interviewed stroke survivors and 73.5% of interviewed hip-fracture survivors received inpatient rehabilitation. These proportions describe service receipt among the interviewed patients. Incomplete discharge recommendations limited the investigators' ability to distinguish failure to refer from failure to receive a service. The accompanying commentary called for measuring function across transitions as well as recording referral and receipt. [Zucker et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1186/2045-4015-2-27), Results, Table 1 and Discussion; [Resnik, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1186/2045-4015-2-28), commentary on discharge documentation and outcome assessment.


## Summary

**Frameworks**
- Nagi model (1960s): active pathology → impairment → functional limitation → disability (p818).
- WHO ICF (2001): biopsychosocial — body functions/structures, activities, participation (p818).

**Evaluation**
- SPPB (gait speed + chair stands + balance): worse scores → falls, NH placement, mortality; UAB Life-Space Assessment = mobility over the preceding month (p820).
- Assess rehab potential after acute illness resolves — delirium, hemodynamic instability, occult fracture, acute infection may delay referral to rehabilitation (p821).

**Sites of care (Medicare)**
- **US Medicare inpatient rehabilitation** includes 24-hour rehabilitation nursing and generally **3 hours of therapy per day on at least 5 days/week**, or **15 hours in 7 consecutive days** in documented cases. Multiple therapy disciplines participate, including PT or OT (p822; [CMS inpatient rehabilitation criteria](https://www.cms.gov/training-education/medicare-learning-networkr-mln/compliance/medicare-provider-compliance-tips/inpatient-rehabilitation-hospitals)).
- SNF: 24-hr nursing, no required minimum therapy intensity (p822).
- Home health ("homebound"): face-to-face encounter **within 90 days before or 30 days after** start; recertified **every 60 days** (p822).

**Team**
- Interdisciplinary rehab team includes easily-forgotten members: chaplain/spiritual care provider (spiritual support to patient and family) and dietician (assesses nutritional status, alters diet to maximize nutrition) (**Table 55-4**, p823).

**Exercise prescription**
- Strength: **60% to 80%** of max lifting ability; older beginners start at **~40% to 50%** (p824).
- Aerobic: usually **≥20 minutes** at **50% to 80%** VO2max; beginners start with **5 to 10 minutes**; needs **three to five times per week** (p824).
- Flexibility: **at least 90 degrees** knee flexion for stairs; **120 to 150 degrees** shoulder flexion/abduction to reach overhead (p825).
- CIMT: **4 to 6 hours/day** forced use of affected limb; greater upper-extremity motor gains than conventional treatment, but the between-group differences were not significant at 6 months (p825).
- Sarcopenia: **60% to 80% of 1RM**, **two to three times a week** (p832).

**Modalities**
- MIRE: **890-nm**, **30-min × 3/week × 12 treatments**. Most studies showed no improvement in sensation over placebo (p826).
- "Cool" pool **<90°F** for heat-intolerant patients (e.g., MS) (p826).

**Adaptive aids**
- Canes: opposite the involved leg; "up with the good, down with the bad" on stairs (p827).
- **Table 55-5** (p828): power wheelchair needs cognitive ability to operate safely (controls don't require intact UEs) — may be beneficial in neurologic disease (high-level SCI, MS, ALS) or multiple-limb amputation; rollator is good for outside walking because of large wheels and has a seat for resting.
- Diabetes and severe diabetic foot disease: Medicare covers **one pair** of therapeutic shoes and inserts or shoe modifications **each year** (p829).

**Condition-specific rehab**
- Pulmonary: exercise **three times a week or more**, target moderate dyspnea (p829).
- Cardiac rehab: only **one-third** of eligible patients participate (**12%** in a cohort of more than 600,000 Medicare patients) (p830).
  - CR vs. none: mortality **2.3% vs. 5.3%**; benefit sustained at 5 years, **16% vs. 25%** (p830).
  - **≥25 sessions** → **20% lower** 5-yr mortality vs. fewer sessions (p830).
  - Heart failure: stable **at least 3 to 4 weeks** before starting; CR → **28%** lower mortality/hospitalization, **29%** lower death rate, peak VO2 **+15% to 30%**, symptoms/ADLs/well-being **+15% to 50%**; QoL gains seen as early as **2 months** (p830).
- **PAD with claudication.** The book advises intermittent walking **to the onset of pain** to increase walking distance before claudication begins (p825). It describes a program lasting **at least 6 months** and reports better gains with **treadmill walking than strength training** (p830).
  > Current practice (not the book): supervised exercise alternates walking to moderate-to-maximum claudication with rest, working toward 30–45 minutes of active walking per session, at least 3 sessions weekly for at least 12 weeks. [2024 ACC/AHA PAD guideline, Table 14](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001251).
- Lower extremity amputation: **60% to 80%** get phantom limb pain; **~10%** of those with phantom pain rate it severe enough to be disabling (p830).
- Stroke: rehab can begin as early as **24 to 48 hours** poststroke once the patient is medically stable (p831).
  - Dysphagia can be silent in **up to one-third of patients with dysphagia**; aphasia in **one-third** (p831).
  - Bladder incontinence **50% to 70%** month 1, back to general-population levels by **6 months** (p831).
  - Shoulder pain **34% to 84%**; depression **15% to 70%** (p831).
- Post-THA (posterolateral): may need to avoid **more than 90 degrees** hip flexion early (p832).

**Putting the rest of the chapter into a plan**

- A diagnosis identifies the disease. A functional history identifies the task that has become difficult and the circumstances in which it fails. Two patients who cannot walk the same distance may be limited by dyspnea or by pain on loading a joint. The impairment, intervention and suitable destination can differ (p818–820).
- The team should set a measurable activity goal with the patient and family. Exercise to improve strength or range is most useful when it serves a task such as rising from a chair, reaching a shelf or transferring into a bath. Persistent symptoms after a session prompt reconsideration of its dose (p822–825).
- Heat, cold and electrical stimulation serve different purposes. Impaired circulation, sensation or cognition can make a thermal treatment unsafe. Buoyancy can assist movement, whereas whole-body warming can be poorly tolerated. The chapter distinguishes stimulation for pain from stimulation for muscle recruitment and wound healing (p825–827).
- Adapt the environment as well as the person. A bathing aid, an appropriate toilet seat or a device for dressing may restore independence in a specific task. Assess whether the patient can operate it and whether it fits at home. Electronic controls and communication aids can help when movement or speech limits participation (p827–829).
- In Parkinsonism, auditory rhythm or visual cues can reduce freezing for some patients. In osteoarthritis, strengthening can improve function and reduce pain, but the exercise that provokes pain should be modified or avoided. Following amputation, previous walking ability, the energy demands of prosthetic gait and capacity to learn matter when planning mobility (p830–832).
- At discharge, residual impairments can limit activity and expose the patient to further complications of inactivity. A transition to feasible community exercise extends the gains of a time-limited rehabilitation episode (p832–833).

## Recall

**1. What are the four components of the Nagi disablement model?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Active pathology → impairment → functional limitation → disability (p818).

</details>

**2. What three domains of human function does the WHO ICF model describe?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Body functions/structures, activities, and participation, shaped by environmental and personal contextual factors (p818).

</details>

**4. What duration, intensity, and frequency of aerobic exercise does the book describe?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Continuous activity usually performed for at least 20 minutes at 50% to 80% of maximal oxygen consumption, three to five times per week; several shorter sessions (ie, 10 minutes) per day have shown similar benefit to one 30-minute session (p824).

</details>

**6. What is constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT) and its typical daily dose?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The stronger/less-affected limb is constrained (cast or mitt) to force use of the affected limb; typically 4–6 hours/day of therapy (p825).

</details>

**7. What is the MIRE protocol, and how did sensation after treatment compare with placebo?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

890-nm near-infrared light pads, 30-minute sessions, three times a week, 12 treatments total; the majority of studies show it is no more effective than placebo in improving sensation (studies are conflicting) (p826).

</details>

**8. In which hand should a cane be held, and why?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In the hand opposite the involved extremity — this preserves a normal gait pattern; using it on the same side turns it into a brace and limits hip/knee ROM on the involved side (p827).

</details>

**10. What therapy-intensity requirement distinguishes a Medicare-certified inpatient rehab facility from a skilled nursing facility?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A US Medicare inpatient rehabilitation facility generally provides 3 hours of therapy on at least 5 days/week, or 15 hours over 7 consecutive days in documented cases. A skilled nursing facility has no equivalent fixed session-intensity requirement (p822, CMS inpatient rehabilitation coverage criteria).

</details>
