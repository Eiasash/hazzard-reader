# Chapter 69 Study: Management of Common Nonpain Symptoms

## Summary

**Symptom burden**
- Multiple nonpain symptoms are common with multimorbidity and may impair function beyond what individual diagnoses predict; screen routinely within geriatric assessment (p1071).
- A nationally representative study found at least **3** symptoms in **28%** of older adults; higher burden accompanied more falls and reduced physical function (p1071).

**Fatigue assessment**
- Ask about onset, daily pattern, aggravating/relieving factors and effects on activity and relationships. Review sleep, medications and depression (p1073).
- Consider anemia, infection, electrolyte problems, malignancy and other systemic causes; new fatigue can reflect MI or heart failure. Let findings guide investigation (p1073).
- Fatigability relates perceived fatigue to a defined activity; this can standardize assessment but is harder to use routinely (p1072–1073).

**Management of fatigue**
- Counseling, functional training and correcting vitamin D deficiency have shown some promise; improving sleep may help (p1073).
- Protein supplementation, thyroid supplementation for subclinical hypothyroidism and testosterone have not shown promise in the studies summarized here (p1073).
- Psychostimulant evidence is mixed. Methylphenidate can cause hypertension and tachycardia; avoid psychostimulants with known cardiovascular disease or arrhythmias (p1073).

**Breathlessness**
- Patient self-report is the most appropriate measure; breathlessness need not correlate with hypoxia, hypercarbia or tachypnea (p1073).
- Treat the cause when known, while using a fan, breathing techniques, posture, relaxation, anxiety management and energy conservation (p1074).
- Oxygen improves outcomes in hypoxic COPD. In nonhypoxic patients, a cited trial found no dyspnea advantage over cannula-delivered air (p1074).
- Noninvasive ventilation may help selected patients, but mask intolerance and anxiety can cause discontinuation (p1074).

**Drugs & exam traps**
- Opioids may help refractory dyspnea after optimal disease treatment, but evidence is inconsistent. Morphine metabolites can accumulate in renal impairment and cause neurotoxicity (p1074).
- Cognitive adverse effects may outweigh breathing relief for some patients. Choose symptom treatment around individual priorities for function, cognition, comfort and survival (p1074–1075).
