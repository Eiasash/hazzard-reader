# Chapter 14 Study: Models of Hospital and Outpatient Care

## Summary

**Choose a model that fits**
- Define the local problem, population, patient goals, team and payment environment; measure baseline outcomes and implementation fidelity (p191-194).
- Models aim to preserve function and quality of life in the least restrictive safe setting; sustaining and spreading them requires leadership, resources and outcome feedback (p191, p203-204).

**Hospital and perioperative care**
- ACE combines a supportive environment, interdisciplinary assessment, functional care and early discharge planning; ACE consultation applies its principles without a dedicated unit (p194-197).
- HELP addresses cognition, sleep, mobility, vision, hearing and hydration; the described target is age **70+** with at least one delirium risk factor (p198).
- NICHE develops nursing expertise through geriatric resource nurses and evidence-based care processes (p199).
- Hospital VA STRIDE provides supervised walking after early assessment; selection excludes patients for whom the protocol is unsuitable (p199).
- AGS CoCare: Ortho co-manages hip fractures; GSV sets geriatric surgical standards; POSH coordinates geriatrics, surgery and anesthesia for elective surgery (p200).

**Community and home models**
- GRACE combines primary care with nurse-practitioner/social-worker support for low-income older adults (p200-201).
- PACE requires age **55+**, state-certified nursing-home eligibility, residence in the service area and ability to remain safely in the community with services (p201).
- STEADI screens, assesses modifiable fall risks and intervenes; outpatient STRIDE is a separate nurse-led fall-injury prevention model (p202).
- Home-Based Primary Care serves severe disabling chronic illness; Independence at Home targets high-risk, high-cost patients with limited mobility and coordinated longitudinal care (p202-203).
- CAPABLE combines occupational therapy, nursing and home repairs to improve function and support aging in place (p203).
- UCLA dementia care uses specialist-supported nurse-practitioner co-management to support patients and caregivers; Gerofit offers exercise for eligible older veterans (p203).
- Fit services to patient abilities and exclusion criteria; similar program names do not imply identical populations or evidence (p199, p202-203).
- Sustain the model with an advisory team including a patient, outcome reporting, staff development and plans for dissemination (p204).

### What the outcome tells you

In the ACE studies, assess the ability to function at discharge as well as length of stay. Patients on ACE units had less disability at discharge and were less likely to leave for skilled nursing care than those receiving usual care. In HELP, fewer delirious days and episodes did not translate into lower severity or recurrence in the comparison described. Fidelity matters when interpreting both a successful trial and a disappointing local result (p194, p198–199).

GRACE paired an in-home assessment with interdisciplinary review and a plan worked out with the primary care clinician. In the high-risk group, reduced emergency and hospital use contributed to lower costs by the **second year**. CAPABLE takes a different route to remaining at home. The nurse and occupational therapist identify goals with the older adult, and a worker makes practical changes to the home so those tasks become possible (p196, p200–201, p203).

## Exam traps

- A consultation can bring ACE methods to an ordinary ward. The dedicated unit also changes the ward environment and daily routines (p194–197).
- Distinguish a fall-risk screen from an intervention that actually addresses the patient's identified risks (p202).
- PACE enrollment requires safe community living with program support at enrollment even though nursing-home-level care is needed (p201).

## Recall drill

**1. What should a hospital measure before introducing a geriatrics model?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The local problem and its baseline outcomes. It can then track outcomes over time and check whether the team delivered the intended components. (p191–194)</details>

**2. Which four elements identify the ACE approach?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A prepared environment, interdisciplinary functional care, medical review and discharge planning that begins on admission. (p194–195)</details>

**3. Why ask about dressing and walking at admission to an ACE unit?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The aim includes maintaining or restoring independence. Admission function informs bedside care and what help will be needed after discharge. (p194)</details>

**4. How can a hospital apply ACE when its older patients are scattered across wards?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An interdisciplinary consultation model brings ACE review and recommendations to those patients without moving all of them to a dedicated unit. (p197)</details>

**5. Which risks does HELP address besides impaired cognition?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sleep deprivation, immobility, poor vision, poor hearing and dehydration. (p198)</details>

**6. Which delirium outcomes did the HELP comparison improve?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Patients spent fewer days delirious and had fewer episodes. Severity and recurrence did not fall in that comparison. (p199)</details>

**7. Which model develops nursing expertise through resource nurses?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>NICHE. It supports bedside nurses in using geriatric assessment and evidence-based care within their own hospital. (p199)</details>

**8. How do the two STRIDE programs differ?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The VA inpatient program promotes early supervised walking. The outpatient fall-injury program assesses individual risks and works with primary care on an intervention plan. (p199–200, p202)</details>

**9. Which perioperative model fits an older adult admitted with a hip fracture?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>AGS CoCare: Ortho brings medical and orthopedic teams together. POSH is described for patients preparing for elective surgery. (p200)</details>

**10. Who delivers the core community assessment in GRACE?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A nurse practitioner and social worker work with primary care and the interdisciplinary team to plan care for low-income older adults. (p200–201)</details>

**11. What are the four PACE enrollment criteria?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Age <strong>55 or older</strong>, residence in the service area, state certification of nursing-home-level need and ability to remain safely in the community with PACE services. (p201)</details>

**12. Which steps distinguish STEADI from advice simply to be careful?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Screen for risk, assess contributors such as gait, medication or home hazards, then intervene on the modifiable findings. (p202)</details>

**13. Which program pairs home modifications with nursing and occupational therapy?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>CAPABLE. Repairs or adaptations target the daily tasks the person wants to manage at home. (p196, p203)</details>

**14. What distinguishes home-based primary care from a single home safety assessment?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It provides ongoing medical care and coordination for patients with severe disabling chronic illness who have difficulty accessing office-based care. (p202–203)</details>

**15. A model produces little change after transfer to a new hospital. What should be checked before dismissing it?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Check the fit with the local population and whether core components were delivered. Review baseline and follow-up measures with the people delivering and receiving care. (p191–194, p204)</details>
