# Chapter 29 Study: Surgical Quality and Outcomes

## Summary

**Define success with the patient**
- Many older adults value independence and quality of life as much as, or more than, survival (p399).
- Recovery includes physical, cognitive, psychological, emotional, social and economic function, sometimes over a year or longer (p399).
- Ask what matters most: cancer control, continued independence or symptom relief may lead to different decisions, including foregoing surgery (p404).
- Short stays, complication rates and readmissions remain useful, but do not fully describe a successful outcome (p399).

**Estimate and reduce risk**
- Frailty predicts perioperative morbidity and mortality better than chronological age alone; it need not correlate with anesthetic physical-status scores (p399-400).
- Appropriately selected older adults can benefit even from high-risk procedures; age alone should not decide access (p400).
- Anticipate delirium, dehydration, falls/fractures, failure to thrive and pressure injury as geriatric surgical complications (p400).
- Geriatric-focused care and co-management are associated with shorter stays and fewer complications and deaths (p399-401).
- Include living situation, falls, mobility aids, cognition, surrogate consent and palliative needs in risk discussions (p403).

**Follow recovery beyond discharge**
- Loss of independence includes declining ADLs or mobility and increased home support or institutional care (p401-402).
- In one surgical cohort, **60%** had some loss of independence at discharge; this was not a universal prognosis (p402).
- Readmissions and intercurrent illness can delay functional recovery; reassess function after these events (p401).
- Delirium is associated with major complications, institutional discharge and mortality; the chapter reports prevention in up to **40%** with perioperative care adjustments (p402).
- Delirium and postoperative cognitive dysfunction share risks, but their relationship over time remains uncertain (p402).
- Quality of life may exceed baseline even after complications or functional decline; discuss expected trajectories and time at home (p402-403).
- Use an individualized plan spanning preoperative optimization, the operation and longitudinal recovery (p403).
