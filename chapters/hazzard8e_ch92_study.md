# Chapter 92 Study: Gastrointestinal Malignancies

**Study summary only - read the full chapter in the book, pp 1435-1454.**

Fast build from the book; not lane-reviewed.

## Summary

### Epidemiology & diagnosis
- Older adults are underrepresented in treatment trials; consider function, comorbidity, toxicity and social support, and refer early for oncology input. (p. 1435)
<a id="p1437"></a>
- Older adults appear to have more right-sided CRC, obstruction and perforation; stage distribution is similar to younger adults. (p. 1437)
- Individualize CRC screening; stopping may be reasonable when life expectancy is shorter than the **5-10 years** for polyp-to-cancer progression. (p. 1440)

### Management
- Localized colon cancer: resection; adequate nodal evaluation examines at least **12 nodes**. Higher-volume hospitals had lower operative mortality in cited older cohorts. (p. 1440)
- Resected stage III colon cancer: adjuvant fluoropyrimidines benefit older adults; added oxaliplatin benefit is less certain in older subgroups and neuropathy may worsen falls/function. Consider fitness and comorbidities. (p. 1441)
<a id="p1445"></a>
- Pancreatic cancer: avoid assigning a poor prognosis without accurate pathological diagnosis; resection is potentially curative and can be safe in carefully selected older adults. (p. 1445)
<a id="p1446"></a>
- Gastric cancer: gastrectomy is the curative treatment described; most, but not all, studies suggest similar perioperative risk across age groups. (p. 1446)
<a id="p1448"></a>
- Liver-confined HCC: resection, transplantation, ablation or locoregional treatments may be options; geriatric assessment helps selection. (p. 1448)
<a id="p1449"></a>
- Esophageal surgery evidence is mixed: most, but not all, studies suggest age alone is not limiting; assess fitness and cardiopulmonary risk. (p. 1449)

### Drugs & supportive care
- Olaparib **300 mg twice daily**: POLO maintenance after at least **16 weeks** of continuous first-line platinum without progression in germline BRCA-mutated metastatic pancreatic cancer; improved progression-free survival, but interim overall survival did not differ. (p. 1446)
- Gemcitabine: liver-enzyme elevation, rarely hemolytic uremic syndrome; FOLFIRINOX: febrile neutropenia and sensory neuropathy are among its toxicities. (p. 1445)
- Pancreatic palliation may include drainage/stenting for biliary obstruction, lipase for pancreatic insufficiency, and celiac plexus neurolysis for pain. (p. 1446)

### Exam traps
- Poorer overall survival can reflect noncancer deaths; do not equate it automatically with worse cancer-specific survival. (p. 1438)
- Sigmoidoscopy can miss proximal tumors, which are more common in older adults. (p. 1439)

## Drill

**1. Why are age-specific cancer treatment estimates uncertain?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Older adults are underrepresented in trials; function, comorbidities, treatment toxicity and social support must be considered when applying the evidence. (p. 1435)</details>

**2. What CRC presentation differences are described in older adults?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>More right-sided tumors, obstruction and perforation, with a greater likelihood of acute presentation; stage distribution is similar to younger adults. (p. 1437)</details>

**3. Why can overall and cancer-specific survival tell different stories?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Comorbid diseases account for substantial mortality; some investigators found age-related overall-survival differences were no longer significant after excluding noncancer deaths. (p. 1438)</details>

**4. What limitation of sigmoidoscopy matters particularly in older adults?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It misses proximal tumors beyond its reach, and older adults are more likely to have proximal neoplasia. (p. 1439)</details>

**5. What time horizon helps frame stopping CRC screening?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Stopping may be reasonable when life expectancy is shorter than the <strong>5-10 years</strong> for a polyp to progress to cancer. (p. 1440)</details>

**6. What minimum lymph-node count defines adequate evaluation in the cited CRC surgical analysis?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>At least <strong>12 lymph nodes</strong>; inadequate evaluation correlated with inferior survival. (p. 1440)</details>

**7. Is oxaliplatin's adjuvant survival benefit established equally in all older adults?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. Older-subgroup findings are mixed, with several showing no added survival advantage; it can be considered for fit adults with minimal comorbidity. (p. 1441)</details>

**8. Which oxaliplatin toxicity can undermine function and increase falls?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Peripheral neuropathy. (p. 1441)</details>

**9. What pancreatic-cancer diagnostic shortcut can incorrectly assign a poor prognosis?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Omitting accurate tissue confirmation to spare diagnostic discomfort; older patients receive pathological confirmation less frequently and may be misdiagnosed. (p. 1445)</details>

**10. What is the potentially curative treatment for localized pancreatic cancer?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Surgical resection; the chapter supports its safety in carefully selected older adults. (p. 1445)</details>

**11. Which rare serious toxicity is described for gemcitabine?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hemolytic uremic syndrome; liver-enzyme elevations are also described. (p. 1445)</details>

**12. Which patients received olaparib maintenance in POLO, and at what dose?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Patients with germline BRCA-mutated metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma without progression after at least <strong>16 weeks</strong> of continuous first-line platinum-based chemotherapy; olaparib <strong>300 mg twice daily</strong>. (p. 1446)</details>

**13. Did POLO's progression-free benefit establish an overall-survival benefit in its interim analysis?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. Progression-free survival improved, while interim overall survival did not differ. (p. 1446)</details>

**14. What symptom-directed interventions are described for pancreatic cancer?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Biliary stents or percutaneous drainage for obstruction, microencapsulated lipase for pancreatic insufficiency, and possible celiac plexus neurolysis for pain. (p. 1446)</details>

**15. Does the chapter describe age as an absolute exclusion from esophageal surgery?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. Most, but not all, studies suggest age is not limiting; the evidence includes contradictory morbidity and mortality findings, so assess individual fitness and comorbidities. (p. 1449)</details>
