# Chapter 41 Study: Managing the Care of Patients with Multiple Chronic Conditions

## Summary

**Think across conditions**
- Multiple chronic conditions interact with function, treatment burden and quality of life; counting diagnoses alone misses these effects (p605-606).
- Single-disease guidelines may conflict or create an impractical combined regimen (p609-610).
- Older adults with multimorbidity are underrepresented in trials; check applicability, outcomes meaningful to the person and possible interactions (p611).
- Interpret absolute benefit as well as relative effects, and compare time to benefit with health trajectory and time to harm (p611-612).

**Establish goals and capacity for care**
- Ask what matters to the patient, what outcomes they seek and what treatment workload they are willing and able to accept (p612-613).
- Ask permission and preferences for discussion of prognosis; broad functional expectations may help even without a precise estimate (p613).
- Treatment burden itself can reduce adherence. Approximately **40%** of older adults report some burden in the evidence discussed (p613).
- Identify caregivers, assess their needs and connect them with support while respecting the patient's autonomy and privacy (p612).
- Document and share priorities with the whole team; revisit them after illness, functional decline or major life changes (p613).

**Refine and coordinate the plan**
- Review medicines, appointments and self-management tasks together, not one disease at a time (p613).
- Consider stopping burdensome, harmful or goal-discordant care; weigh lag time to benefit against the expected course (p613).
- Deprescribing candidates include drugs without a valid indication, duplication, interactions, excessive dose, adverse effects or unfavorable benefit/cost (p611).
- Agree a taper/stop plan and monitor the consequences; patient and family preferences guide selection (p611).
- Explicit tools such as Beers and STOPP/START help, but do not replace individualized judgment (p610-611).
- Coordinate across clinicians to reduce redundant testing and conflicting recommendations; evidence for care-management programs is mixed (p612).
- Resolve disagreements around shared patient goals, offer alternatives and revisit decisions when appropriate (p613).
