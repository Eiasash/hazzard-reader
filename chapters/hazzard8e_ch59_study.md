# Dementia Including Alzheimer Disease

Establish cognitive decline from the person's usual function, then interpret the pattern, time course and biomarkers together. Treatment and goals change as dependence increases.

## Risk factors and biology

Age is the strongest established risk factor discussed in Hazzard. A family with younger-onset disease in successive generations raises a different question: a pathogenic mutation in APP, PSEN1 or PSEN2 can cause autosomal dominant AD. Among the familial mutations grouped in the chapter, PSEN1 accounts for about 78%. APP maps to chromosome 21, which also explains the relevance of APP dosage in Down syndrome. See the chapter’s discussion of familial mutations (<a href="?chapter=59#p897">pp897–898</a>).

**>6 million Americans**, **>44 million worldwide** have AD or a related dementia (<a href="?chapter=59#p894">p894</a>).

Prevalence rises steeply with age: **~3% (65–74y), 17% (75–84y), 32% (85y+)** (<a href="?chapter=59#p894">p894</a>).

**Late-onset AD accounts for more than 95%** of cases in the chapter's estimate (<a href="?chapter=59#p894">p894</a>). **Familial AD accounts for 1–5%** and is one cause of younger-onset AD. Younger-onset disease can also occur without a causative familial mutation (<a href="?chapter=59#p897">p897</a>).

Familial AD genes: *APP*, *PSEN1*, *PSEN2*. **PSEN1 = 78%** of familial AD mutations (most common) (<a href="?chapter=59#p897">p897</a>); familial AD onset typically **late 40s–early 50s** (<a href="?chapter=59#p898">p898</a>).

Vascular risk factors (high midlife cholesterol/BP, diabetes, metabolic syndrome, obesity, inactivity) all raise AD risk. Midlife obesity RR **1.60** (95% CI 1.34–1.92); midlife physical inactivity RR **1.82** (95% CI 1.19–2.78) (<a href="?chapter=59#p896">p896</a>).

**About 1/3 of AD cases worldwide** might be attributable to potentially modifiable risk factors (<a href="?chapter=59#p896">p896</a>).

> **Current practice: Modifiable dementia risk factors.** The 2024 Lancet Commission estimated a population-attributable fraction of approximately 45% across 14 potentially modifiable risk factors, including high LDL cholesterol and untreated vision loss. This is a population estimate under causal assumptions, not an individual prediction. [Lancet Commission, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39096926/).

TBI: WWII veterans with moderate TBI had **2×** AD risk, severe TBI with loss of consciousness **4×** (<a href="?chapter=59#p896">p896</a>).

Late-life depression history → AD risk **OR 1.65** (95% CI 1.42–1.92) (<a href="?chapter=59#p896">p896</a>); >30% of AD patients develop depression during the illness (may be the first symptom).

Low educational attainment: pooled **RR 1.80** (95% CI 1.45–2.27) for AD (<a href="?chapter=59#p897">p897</a>).

APOE on chromosome 19 modifies susceptibility to late-onset AD. The ε4 allele increases risk in a dose-dependent fashion, while ε2 is relatively protective. An ε4 allele does not establish a diagnosis or make onset inevitable. Distinguish a susceptibility gene from the disease-causing mutations seen in some younger-onset families (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p897">897</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p898">898</a>).

AD = most common neurodegenerative disorder / cause of dementia in older adults.

AD is the most common dementia in the United States, accounting for **50–90%** of all dementia cases (<a href="?chapter=59#p907">p907</a>).

*APOE* ε4: **one copy → 4× risk, two copies → 12× risk** of AD (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>). Framingham: **55%** of ε4 homozygotes vs **27%** heterozygotes vs **9%** noncarriers developed AD by age 85 (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>). ε2 allele is protective.

Hallmarks: amyloid (Aβ) plaques + neurofibrillary tangles (tau); neuritic plaques = subset with dystrophic neurites.

2012 NIA-AA neuropathologic "ABC score" (**A**myloid/**B**raak/**C**ERAD) grades autopsy findings as none/low/intermediate/high (<a href="?chapter=59#p899">p899</a>).

**10–30%** of clinically diagnosed AD-dementia patients lack amyloid/tau pathology at autopsy — clinical diagnosis and biological (autopsy) diagnosis are not the same thing (<a href="?chapter=59#p908">p908</a>). 2018 AT(N) framework reframes AD as a biological (amyloid+tau), not purely clinical, diagnosis.

> **Current practice: Blood biomarkers for symptomatic cognitive impairment.** The 2024 Alzheimer’s Association criteria include validated Core 1 biomarkers of Alzheimer pathology. In 2025 the FDA cleared a plasma pTau217/β-amyloid 1-42 ratio test to aid evaluation of symptomatic adults aged 55 or older in a specialized care setting. Interpret the result with the clinical assessment and other tests; it is intended neither for screening nor as a stand-alone diagnostic test. [Alzheimer’s Association revised criteria, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38934362/) · [FDA blood test clearance, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-clears-first-blood-test-used-diagnosing-alzheimers-disease).

Nonpharmacologic care: exercise, cognitive/social stimulation, safety review (driving, firearms, stove), caregiver support/respite.

Behavioral symptoms: generally try nonpharmacologic measures first unless agitation threatens safety or the person’s living situation (<a href="?chapter=59#p915">p915</a>, see chapter 60).

## Clinical features and function

### Recognizing cognitive decline in daily life

Start with what the patient used to do, what now takes longer and who has had to take over. In mild cognitive impairment (MCI) there is a reported change and objective impairment in at least one domain, judged against age and education. Complex tasks may take longer or require a list, but the patient remains independent. Repeated medication or financial errors that require another person to take responsibility change the functional diagnosis. Functional independence is decisive (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p896">896</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-1">Tables 59-1</a> and <a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-2">59-2</a>).

A single screening score gives a level of performance. Interviewing the patient and someone who knows them establishes whether that level represents decline. An academic may start from a high baseline, and sensory loss or limited schooling may affect performance. To assess abstraction, ask for the relationship between two objects. The chapter describes the role of a MoCA in a wider assessment (<a href="?chapter=59#p901">p901</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p906">906</a>).

A gradual decline over months or years supports probable Alzheimer dementia. Acute fluctuation calls for evaluation of delirium and its cause, followed by reassessment of cognition after recovery. Depression can coexist with AD and may present with irritability, altered appetite or sleep. Ask about previous function and the time course before attributing a change to dementia (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p900">900</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p905">905</a>).

Most common: **amnestic presentation** — progressive recent memory loss, often unrecognized by the patient (<a href="?chapter=59#p900">p900</a>).

Approximately 12% to 15% of people with MCI progress to dementia each year in the studies cited. This is a group rate that warrants follow-up. The Jack clinical staging criteria use a different framework (<a href="?chapter=laws&amp;lawCard=ima-article-aa-2024#law-card-ima-article-aa-2024">Table 6</a>). Stage 2 allows symptoms with objective performance in the expected range. Stage 3 requires objectively impaired performance with independence preserved. Interpret a MoCA score in its normative context (<a href="?chapter=59#p907">p907</a> and the exam's supplied article 4).

Nonamnestic presentations (language, visuospatial, executive dysfunction) are more often missed/delayed in diagnosis.

Late stages: falls, dysphagia, incontinence, immobility; death commonly from pneumonia, urosepsis, dehydration, pressure sores.

## Assessment, differential diagnosis and biomarkers

### Reading the pattern and the biomarkers together

Alzheimer disease usually begins with progressive loss of memory for recent events. Frontotemporal dementia can instead cause early language dysfunction with relative sparing of memory, or early disinhibition, loss of empathy and repetitive behavior. Both presentations can occur in frontotemporal dementia (<a href="?chapter=59#p908">p908</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-5">Table 59-5</a>).

Fluctuating attention, recurrent formed visual hallucinations and spontaneous parkinsonism favor Lewy body disease. A vascular lesion can instead produce an abrupt or stepwise change or a subcortical profile with slowed processing and poor attention. Relate the timing, deficits and imaging to each other. A lacune on a scan alone does not establish vascular dementia or lost independence. Under the VasCog criteria, vascular and Alzheimer contributions may both be present. The infarcts support the vascular component, while the type of tau assay and the APOE genotype matter to a biological attribution (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p908">908</a>; supplied article 2, Boxes 2–3).

NIA-AA dementia criteria (<a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-1">Table 59-1</a>): decline from previous level that interferes with function, not explained by delirium or major psychiatric disorder; history from patient and a knowledgeable informant + objective cognitive assessment; impairment involves at least two cognitive or behavioral domains (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>).

Clinical diagnosis describes the syndrome, while biomarkers can identify pathology. Hazzard's 2018 research framework groups amyloid, pathological tau and evidence of neuronal injury as AT(N). Injury may have causes other than AD. In the later Jack criteria, an accurate plasma p-tau217 test is a Core 1 marker, tau PET is Core 2, and structural atrophy or neurofilament light is less specific. The IWG framework reserves presymptomatic AD for a cognitively normal person with a very high lifetime risk, such as the person with Down syndrome (<a href="?chapter=59#p908">p908</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p910">910</a>; supplied articles 3 and 4).

Interpret CSF and MRI findings in the clinical context. The cognitive history does not establish either result by itself. CSF amyloid deposition is generally associated with lower Aβ42 or a lower Aβ42 to Aβ40 ratio, while AD-related phosphorylated tau rises. MRI can identify a coexisting vascular contribution (<a href="?chapter=59#p906">p906</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p908">908</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p910">910</a>).

NIA-AA MCI criteria (<a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-2">Table 59-2</a>): concern about change noted by the patient, an informant or a clinician; lower performance in ≥1 domain; **preserved independence in functional abilities**; not dementia (<a href="?chapter=59#p896">p896</a>).

Reversible-cause workup: meds (anticholinergic/sedating), depression, hearing loss, OSA, alcohol, vascular disease, delirium.

Labs: B12, folate, TSH, CBC, electrolytes, LFTs, 25-OH vitamin D; HIV/syphilis if atypical; thiamine if heavy alcohol use (<a href="?chapter=59#p906">p906</a>).

Brain CT or MRI recommended in patients with documented cognitive impairment (<a href="?chapter=59#p906">p906</a>).

**MCI noted in up to 15–20%** of older adults; progresses to dementia at **~12–15%/year** (<a href="?chapter=59#p907">p907</a>).

## Treatment and risk reduction

### Choosing symptomatic therapy and recognizing toxicity

Cholinesterase inhibitors raise acetylcholine at synapses. A patient with established Alzheimer dementia may stabilize or decline more slowly. Hazzard reports measurable cognitive improvement in 18% to 48% of treated patients, while most have no readily noticeable improvement. These agents have not been shown to delay progression from MCI to dementia. Distinguish treatment of AD or Lewy body dementia from treatment of memory complaints alone (<a href="?chapter=59#p910">p910</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p912">912</a>).

Ask about pauses, fainting, conduction disease and weight loss before titrating. The medication table associates donepezil with insomnia as well as bradycardia, heart block and syncope. A dose taken with food may reduce gastrointestinal effects. Taking it fasting may worsen gastrointestinal intolerance. Bradycardia is a class concern for the three cholinesterase inhibitors. June 2026 Basic Q102 names Chapter 59 in the official list but gives printed <a href="?chapter=61#p945">p945</a>–<a href="?chapter=61#p946">946</a> in Chapter 61. The donepezil adverse-effect row relevant to the tracing is on <a href="?chapter=59#p912">p912</a> (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p913">913</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-7">Table 59-7</a>).

**AChEIs** (mainstay): **donepezil (Aricept)**, **galantamine (Razadyne)**, **rivastigmine (Exelon)** — the text says start low with dose increases approximately every 2 months (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>), while <a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-7">Table 59-7</a> lists drug-specific steps: donepezil **4–6 wk** before 5→10 mg (the 10→23 mg step after **3 months** on 10 mg) (<a href="?chapter=59#p912">p912</a>), galantamine **≥4 wk** per step (<a href="?chapter=59#p912">p912</a>), rivastigmine **≥2 wk** per oral step or **4–6 wk** for the initial patch step, with a maximum of **13.3 mg/24 h** (<a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-7">Table 59-7</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p913">p913</a>); **18–48%** show cognitive improvement, most others have a plateau or slower cognitive decline (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>). Cautions: GI upset (take with food); bradycardia/AV block/syncope/weight loss are triggers to deprescribe.

Memantine blocks NMDA receptors and is described for moderate-to-severe Alzheimer disease. In a patient unable to tolerate a cholinesterase inhibitor because of gastrointestinal effects or bradycardia, the chapter allows memantine as the initial agent. Headache is listed for memantine. Drug stage, tolerance and symptoms explain the June 2023 and September 2024 answers. In moderate-to-severe Alzheimer disease, the book reports no demonstrated advantage of combined memantine and a cholinesterase inhibitor over either alone for cognition, function or behavior. It calls for more evidence before recommending memantine in earlier disease. The strength of evidence varies with disease severity and combination therapy (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p914">914</a>).

**Memantine (Namenda)** — NMDA-receptor antagonist, approved for **moderate-to-severe AD**; combo with an AChEI has not been shown to be superior to either drug alone (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>).

### Reducing risk, supporting function and interpreting treatment effects

Midlife hypertension, obesity and physical inactivity are associated with later AD. Shared risk factors can accumulate. In the vascular-risk cohort, decline was faster particularly among APOE ε4 carriers. A genetic association does not remove opportunities to address smoking, hearing impairment or vascular risk. Hearing loss made the largest weighted midlife contribution among the factors compared in the 2020 Lancet Commission. Population-attributable estimates describe a population under assumptions, not an individual response to treating hearing loss (<a href="?chapter=59#p895">p895</a>–<a href="?chapter=59#p897">897</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p915">915</a>; Livingston et al., 2020, <a href="?chapter=laws&amp;lawCard=ima-article-aa-2024#law-card-ima-article-aa-2024">Table 2</a>).

Intensive blood-pressure treatment in SPRINT-MIND reduced incident MCI, a different endpoint from a conclusive reduction in dementia. The 2020 Commission emphasizes treating hypertension, while the prevention review distinguishes observational associations from randomized proof that modifying a single exposure prevents dementia. Inherited susceptibility also matters. The 2024 Commission states that overall risk remains modifiable irrespective of APOE status (<a href="?chapter=59#p915">p915</a>; Livingston et al., 2024, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)01296-0">p573</a>; supplied article 5).

**SPRINT-MIND**: SBP goal **120 mmHg** (vs 140) → **19%** reduction in incident MCI (<a href="?chapter=59#p915">p915</a>).

Select activities the person can still perform and enjoys, adapting the physical and cognitive demands to their ability. Frustration with a task that depends on a lost skill is a reason to change the task. Exercise, social activity and cognitive engagement may offer benefit, while respite and support address caregiver strain (<a href="?chapter=59#p915">p915</a>).

Amyloid removal and a clinically important change in function are distinct endpoints. Hazzard describes variable clinical findings and imaging abnormalities with aducanumab. Lecanemab was evaluated in a later trial. At 18 months both group means worsened on CDR-SB, by 1.21 points with lecanemab and 1.66 with placebo. This was 27% less CDR-SB worsening. ARIA-E occurred in 12.6% and ARIA-H in 17.3% of lecanemab-treated participants. With donanemab, iADRS decline over 76 weeks was 35.1% slower in the low/medium-tau subgroup ([TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2, 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2807533)). These group differences represent slower decline (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p914">914</a>; van Dyck et al., <a href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2212948">Table 2</a>).

**Aducanumab** received FDA accelerated approval in June 2021 after trials in MCI due to AD or mild AD dementia (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#book-table-59-7">Table 59-7</a>, <a href="?chapter=59#p914">p914</a>). EMERGE found 22% less worsening on CDR-SB over 18 months. ENGAGE did not show significant benefit. Hazzard reports MRI abnormalities (ARIA-E or ARIA-H) in **34–36%** of high-dose participants, with **about 80% of affected participants asymptomatic** (<a href="?chapter=59#p911">p911</a>).

> **Current practice: Anti-amyloid treatment and imaging risks.** Aducanumab’s US approval was withdrawn on 1 November 2024. Its pooled high-dose trials reported ARIA-E in 35.2%, any ARIA in 41.3%, and no symptoms in 75.8% of participants with ARIA. Lecanemab received traditional US approval in July 2023 and donanemab in July 2024. Treatment begins in amyloid-confirmed mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to Alzheimer disease. Both treatments can cause dangerous ARIA. Discuss APOE ε4 status and test before treatment to assess risk; follow the product-specific MRI schedule. [FDA withdrawal register, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/151146/download) · [FDA lecanemab approval, 2023](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-converts-novel-alzheimers-disease-treatment-traditional-approval) · [FDA donanemab approval, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-adults-alzheimers-disease) · [Salloway et al., 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2786606).

## Advanced dementia and goals of care

Progressive dysphagia, poor intake and infection are common in advanced disease. Discuss prognosis and the patient's goals with the caregiver. In Mitchell's advanced dementia article, FAST 7c entails loss of independent ambulation. Hospice guidelines combine advanced functional decline with at least one qualifying complication. In the circumstances specified in its table, substantial weight loss or serum albumin below 2.5 g/dL can document impaired nutrition. These criteria estimate eligibility and cannot predict precisely whether an individual will die within six months. The September 2024 question supplies the advanced stage and asks which complication qualifies. Mitchell describes the prognosis of advanced dementia (<a href="?chapter=59#p916">p916</a>; Mitchell, <a href="?chapter=laws&amp;lawCard=cited-mitchell-advanced-dementia-2015#law-card-cited-mitchell-advanced-dementia-2015">Table 1</a>).

When pneumonia occurs, consider relief of suffering and possible prolongation of life separately. Antibiotic treatment was associated with longer survival in an observational study, while greater comfort did not follow. Eating problems and infections are the common complications in advanced dementia. Tube feeding has not been shown to extend survival, improve comfort or reduce aspiration in advanced AD. Hand feeding as tolerated and a simplified regimen can align care with comfort (<a href="?chapter=59#p916">p916</a>; Mitchell, clinical complications).

**Feeding tubes are not recommended** in advanced AD — they do not prolong survival or improve comfort, and have not been shown to reduce pressure sores, infection, or aspiration risk (<a href="?chapter=59#p916">p916</a>).

## Key numbers

- Median survival from diagnosis to death: **7–10 years** (<a href="?chapter=59#p901">p901</a>).

- Cerebral amyloid angiopathy co-occurs in **80–90%** of AD patients (sporadic CAA) — can cause lobar hemorrhage/microbleeds (<a href="?chapter=59#p898">p898</a>).

<p dir="rtl"><strong>מקבל החלטות זמני.</strong> תיקון 16 לחוק זכויות החולה מסדיר מסלול לטיפול רפואי דחוף בבגיר שאינו יכול לתת הסכמה מדעת גם לאחר התאמות רלוונטיות. רופא מומחה קובע את הדחיפות ואת יכולת המטופל להסכים. העובד הסוציאלי בודק מינויים, ייפוי כוח והנחיות קודמות, ומברר את הסכמת המשפחה. אם אין הנחיה קודמת מתאימה, סדר הקדימויות הוא בן או בת זוג, ילד, הורה ואח, ובני המשפחה יכולים להסכים על בחירת בן משפחה אחר. המינוי מתועד ומאושר במוסד הרפואי לתקופה של עד שישה חודשים. לפני כל טיפול בודקים מחדש אם המטופל מסוגל להסכים בעצמו. משרד הבריאות, חטיבת הרפואה, <a href="https://emda.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C-%D7%94%D7%97%D7%9C%D7%98%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%96%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%99-%D7%9C%D7%A2%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%A8%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%90%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D.pdf">״מקבל החלטות זמני לעניינים רפואיים״</a>, 15.02.2024, סעיפים 1–4, 7 ונספחים א׳–ב׳, לפי סעיפים 14א–14ג לחוק זכויות החולה.</p>

<p dir="rtl"><strong>נהיגה.</strong> הרופא מעריך אם הפגיעה הקוגניטיבית או התפקודית יוצרת בפועל סיכון בנהיגה, בהתחשב בחומרה, בתפקוד ובסוג הרישיון. כשמחליטים לדווח למרב״ד, מזהירים את הנהג, ממליצים להפסיק לנהוג עד הבדיקה ומתעדים את ההסבר. הדיווח עצמו אינו פוסל מיד את הרישיון. לאחר הבדיקה המרב״ד ממליץ לרשות הרישוי. משרד הבריאות, חוזר מנכ״ל <a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mk06-2023/he/files_circulars_mk_mk06_2023.pdf">6/2023</a>, סעיפים 4–5, 7, 10–11. העדכון מ־<a href="https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mk06-2023/he/files_circulars_mk_mk-275558923.pdf">24.08.2023</a> ביטל את הטופס שצורף לחוזר והפנה לטופס המעודכן בפורטל המרב״ד.</p>

<p dir="rtl"><strong>רצף הטיפול והמשפחה.</strong> התוכנית הלאומית משנת 2013 המליצה להרחיב גישה לאבחון בקהילה ובמרפאות זיכרון, להכין לאחר האבחון תוכנית טיפול עם האדם ועם משפחתו, ולתאם את שירותי הבריאות והרווחה. בשלבים מתקדמים היא הציעה לשלב טיפול פליאטיבי בבית ולהכין את המשפחה לסיבוכים ולהחלטות הצפויות. מרכזי יום, נופשונים וייעוץ לבני המשפחה נועדו לתת מענה גם לצורכי המטפלים. <a href="https://brookdale-web.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2018/01/Dementia_strategy_heb_ES.pdf">התוכנית הלאומית להתמודדות עם מחלת האלצהיימר ודמנציות אחרות</a>, תקציר מנהלים, 2013, עמ׳ ii–iv, סעיפים 2–4.</p>

<p dir="rtl"><strong>שני נתונים מישראל והאוכלוסייה שאליה הם מתייחסים.</strong> ברוקדייל דיווח ב־2024 שחולים מתחת לגיל 65 היו פחות מ־2% מכלל החולים בדמנציה בישראל. במחקר נפרד שפורסם ב־2015 רואיינו 117 בני משפחה שטיפלו בזקנים עם דמנציה מתקדמת שחיו בבית. 74% מהמרואיינים תיארו את עומס הטיפול ככבד או כבד מאוד. הנתון השני מתאר את המשפחות שרואיינו במחקר הקהילתי. ברוקדייל, <a href="https://brookdale.jdc.org.il/publication/dealing-with-early-onset-dementia/">דמ־24־027</a>, תקציר הפרסום, ״רקע״. ברוקדייל, <a href="https://brookdale.jdc.org.il/publication/assessing-quality-end-life-care-older-persons-advanced-dementia-living-community/">דמ־15־652</a>, תקציר הפרסום, פסקת השיטה והממצא על עומס המשפחה.</p>
