# Chapter 71 Study: Effective Communication Strategies For Patients with Serious Illness

## Summary

**Prepare and understand**
- Goals-of-care conversations are often wanted but do not happen; conduct them over time, not only during a crisis (p1089-1090).
- Arrange privacy, adequate time and the patient's chosen supports; clarify relevant medical information beforehand (p1090).
- Ask what the person understands and how much information and decision-making involvement they want (p1090-1091).
- Use ASK-TELL-ASK: establish understanding, explain clearly in small portions, then check comprehension (p1090-1091).

**Values, emotion and decisions**
- Explore hopes, worries, meaning and acceptable quality of life before matching treatments to goals (p1091).
- Respond to emotion with NURSE: name, understand, respect, support and explore (p1091).
- Explain options in relation to the person's priorities, check understanding and offer a recommendation when helpful (p1091-1092).
- Summarize the plan, next steps and goals; reassure the person that care and support will continue (p1092).
- Acknowledge prognostic uncertainty, use ranges and distinguish population estimates from an individual's course (p1093-1094).

**Adapt communication**
- For hearing loss, face the patient, reduce background noise, speak clearly and slowly, support lip-reading and use hearing aids/amplification (p1092).
- For memory difficulty, offer small, separate pieces of information and repeat as needed (p1092).
- Include supporters while continuing to address the patient directly and allowing time to answer (p1093).
- With incapacity, review prior wishes; use substituted judgment when possible and best interests when preferences cannot reasonably be established (p1093).

**Difficult situations**
- SPIKES structures difficult news: setting, perception, invitation, knowledge, empathy, then summary/strategy (p1095).
- Explain the expected course and feeding options in advanced dementia; the chapter finds tube feeding does not improve survival, aspiration or pressure-injury outcomes (p1094).
- Discuss resuscitation, intensive care and transitions before crisis; hospice and symptom-focused care can be explored in relation to the patient's goals (p1095).
- Document and revisit preferences as illness and circumstances change (p1095).
