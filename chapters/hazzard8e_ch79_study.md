# Chapter 79 Study: Hypertension

## Summary

**Mechanisms & risk**
- Arterial stiffness drives age-associated systolic and pulse-pressure elevation; reduced baroreflex sensitivity increases variability and postural/postprandial hypotension (p1219, p1221).
- Renal sodium retention, sympathetic activity, central adiposity and inactivity contribute. High blood pressure should not be dismissed as normal aging (p1219, p1221).

**Diagnosis**
- Measure after at least **5 minutes** seated rest with feet on the floor, appropriate cuff and standardized technique (p1222).
- Diagnose from repeated averages, not a single value; the chapter describes multiple readings over separate visits or **24-hour** ambulatory/home measurements (p1219, p1222).
- The chapter's cited guideline lowered the systolic diagnostic threshold to **130 mm Hg** (p1220).
- Assess renal disease/proteinuria, retinal and cardiac effects, cardiovascular risks, frailty and comorbidity (p1223).

**Targets & management**
- For ambulatory community-living older adults, the cited guideline uses a systolic goal of **130 mm Hg**; high comorbidity burden or limited life expectancy calls for patient-informed judgment (p1223).
- SPRINT compared systolic targets below **120** versus **140 mm Hg**. In participants at least **75**, intensive treatment reduced the primary cardiovascular outcome by **34%** and all-cause mortality by **33%** (p1223).
- Support sodium reduction, weight management and physical activity; lifestyle changes remain useful alongside medication (p1224).
- Select medication using comorbid indications, tolerability, cost and regimen simplicity; achieved BP reduction matters more than class alone (p1224–1225).

**Drugs & cautions**
- Thiazide-type diuretics, ACE inhibitors/ARBs and calcium-channel antagonists are options; ACE inhibitors/ARBs are often used with diabetes or heart failure (p1225).
- Beta blockers are not initial therapy solely for hypertension in older adults without a compelling indication (p1225).
- Monitor supine and upright pressure: treatment-related hypotension may present as weakness or fatigue rather than classic dizziness (p1223).

**Exam traps**
- If seated systolic pressure cannot fall below **130 mm Hg** without postural hypotension, consider a standing-pressure-based target (p1223).
- SPRINT's dementia reduction was not statistically significant, although mild cognitive impairment was reduced; do not describe definite dementia prevention (p1223).
- Individual treatment goals still matter even when trials demonstrate benefit in community-dwelling frail subgroups (p1223).
