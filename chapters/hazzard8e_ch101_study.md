# Chapter 101 Study: Rheumatoid Arthritis and Other Autoimmune Diseases

## Summary

**RA — definition & classification**
- 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria (**Table 101-1**): joint involvement + serology + acute-phase reactants + duration; **score > 6** = definite RA (p1608)
- Criteria cohorts had mean age **46–58 yr** — usefulness for diagnosing **LORA (late-onset RA, >60 yr)** is unvalidated (p1607)
- Anti-CCP: **70–78%** sensitive, **88–96%** specific for RA — more specific than IgM RF, and unlike RF does not rise with normal aging (p1608)

**RA — epidemiology**
- US/Western prevalence **~0.8%**; **0.2–0.3%** China/Japan/Greece/Africa; up to **7%** in some American-Indian/Alaska Native populations (p1608)
- Women affected **2–3×** more than men overall, but this **gender parity disappears in old age** (p1608) — LORA occurs with roughly equal M:F (p1610)
- Peak incidence in the **6th–8th decades** (p1608); RF prevalence rises with age to **~30% by age 90** (p1610)

**LORA clinical pattern (Table 101-2)** (p1610)
- Acute onset, **oligoarticular (2–6 joints)**, large/proximal joints, systemic symptoms (weight loss), absent nodules, sicca common, high ESR, often RF-negative
- **RS3PE** syndrome: remitting seronegative symmetric synovitis with pitting edema; older men **2–3:1**, negative RF/ANA, responds to low-dose prednisone (p1610–1611)
- In one small study, anti-CCP **65%** positive in LORA vs **0%** in PMR or healthy older adults — may be useful to separate LORA from PMR (p1611)

**RA — evaluation**
- ACR functional class I–IV (**Table 101-3**) (p1611)
- >10 yr RA duration → risk of **cervical spine/atlantoaxial instability** — assess before intubation (p1612)
- **Vectra DA**: FDA-approved 12-protein multibiomarker blood test for RA disease activity, complements (does not replace) clinical judgment (p1612)
- Rheumatoid nodules in up to **40%** of seropositive White patients; pleuritis/pericarditis in **20–30%** (usually asymptomatic) (p1613)

**RA — pharmacotherapy (Table 101-4, Table 101-5)**
- Methotrexate monotherapy = first-line for DMARD-naïve moderate-high activity, started **without short- or long-term glucocorticoids** (2021 ACR guideline) (p1615, p1616)
- Hydroxychloroquine dose **5 mg/kg**; **annual ocular screening**, preferably with OCT — avoid in macular degeneration/inoperable dense cataracts (p1614)
- Biologic/tsDMARD added if not at target on max-tolerated methotrexate, preferred over triple csDMARD therapy (p1615, p1616)

**RA — biologics safety & steroids**
- CONSIDER mnemonic for biologic safety in older adults (**Table 101-6**): Comorbidities, Opportunistic infections, Novel presentation of side effects, Screening, Immunization, Drug dosing and interactions, Evaluate, Remaining lifespan (p1618)
- Glucocorticoid dose-response in Medicare beneficiaries **>75 yr**: **≤5 mg, 5–10 mg, >10 mg/day** → **20%, 32%, 47%** increased serious-infection risk vs **13%** in non-users (p1618)
- TB screening (skin test or IGRA + CXR) required before anticytokine therapy; anti-TNF associated with **~4×** malignancy rate in one meta-analysis (p1617)

**Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)**
- **Late-onset lupus (LOL)**: onset after **age 55**, ~**10%** of all lupus cases (p1619)
- UK GPRD: peak incidence in women **50–54 yr**, in men **70–74 yr** (p1619)
- 2019 EULAR/ACR criteria (**Table 101-7**) use **ANA ≥1:80** as entry criterion (p1620)
- LOL diagnostic delay averages **2–3 years**; female:male ratio **4:1** in LOL vs **9:1** in younger-onset lupus (p1621)
- LOL symptom frequencies (**Table 101-8**): arthritis/arthralgia **60%**, rash **47%**, cytopenias **45%**, interstitial pneumonitis **41%**, serositis **32%**, glomerulonephritis only **24%** — milder, less classic disease than YOL (p1621)
- **Belimumab** (anti-BAFF) — first lupus drug in its class to get FDA approval, **2011**, indication expanded to lupus nephritis **2021**; voclosporin (oral calcineurin inhibitor) also approved for active lupus nephritis, role in older patients untested (p1621–1622)

**Sjögren syndrome**
- Second commonest autoimmune disease after RA; prevalence **0.04–4.8%**; peak onset **4th–5th decade**, **9:1** F:M (p1622)
- Schirmer test positive if **<5 mm** wetting in **5 min**; minor salivary gland biopsy positive if **>1 focus (≥50 lymphocytes) per 4 mm²**; normal saliva production **≥1.5 mL/15 min** (p1622)
- Extraglandular disease in **~60%** (**Table 101-9**) — nonerosive arthritis/arthralgia is commonest (p1622–1623)
- Sjögren patients are **44×** more likely to develop lymphoma than matched controls, almost always B-cell, often t(14;18) (p1626)

**Drug-induced lupus (DIL) (Table 101-10)**
- >100 drugs implicated; incidence **~15,000–20,000/yr**, **30,000–50,000** affected in the US (p1624–1625)
- Procainamide: **1/3** of patients on it >1 yr develop symptoms, almost all ANA+ by 2 yr; hydralazine: **<20%** develop DIL, risk dose-dependent (**>200 mg/day** or **>100 g** cumulative) (p1625)
- >90% of PIL/HIL patients have antihistone antibodies; IFN-α causes autoimmunity in up to **19%** of treated patients (p1625)

**Rheumatic syndromes & cancer**
- **Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy** = most common malignancy-associated rheumatic syndrome, almost always from metastatic **lung cancer** (p1625)
- Methotrexate-associated lymphoma: **~50%** harbor EBV genome, often regresses on drug cessation (p1626)
- Azathioprine + cyclosporin A in RA → **2×** lymphoma risk; renal transplant on cyclosporin A → **50-fold** increased NHL risk (p1626)
- Aromatase inhibitors: as many as **half** of women get subjective joint achiness or pain, rarely frank arthritis (p1626–1627)

**COVID-19 and rheumatic disease (Table 101-11)**
- mRNA vaccines preferred over J&J; immunosuppressed patients get an extra dose ≥28 days after primary series (p1627–1628)
- Rituximab: vaccinate **2–4 weeks before** next anticipated dose; most other agents held **1–2 weeks** post-vaccination if disease activity allows — but the Task Force reached **no consensus** on interrupting TNF/IL-6R/IL-1R/IL-17/IL-12,23/IL-23 inhibitors around vaccination (Table 101-11, p1628)

## Drill

**1. What total score on the 2010 ACR/EULAR RA classification criteria defines "definite RA"?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A score greater than 6 (Table 101-1) (p1608).

</details>

**2. What are the sensitivity and specificity of anti-CCP antibodies for RA?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

70–78% sensitive, 88–96% specific — and unlike RF, anti-CCP incidence does not rise with normal aging (p1608).

</details>

**3. How does the male:female ratio of RA differ between typical (younger-onset) RA and LORA?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Typical RA affects women 2–3× more than men, but this gender parity disappears in old age (p1608) — LORA occurs with roughly equal frequency in men and women (p1610).

</details>

**4. What joint pattern and demographic features characterize LORA (Table 101-2)?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Acute-onset oligoarticular disease (2–6 joints), involvement of large/proximal joints, systemic symptoms (e.g., weight loss), absence of rheumatoid nodules, common sicca symptoms, and a roughly 1:1 male:female ratio (p1610).

</details>

**5. What is RS3PE syndrome and how does it typically respond to treatment?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Remitting seronegative symmetric synovitis with pitting edema — affects older men (2–3:1), RF/ANA negative, high ESR, and generally responds well to low-dose prednisone (p1610–1611).

</details>

**6. How useful is anti-CCP antibody testing for distinguishing LORA from polymyalgia rheumatica?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In one small study, 65% of LORA patients were anti-CCP positive versus 0% of PMR patients or healthy older adults — a potentially useful discriminator (p1611).

</details>

**7. What RA-specific risk requires evaluation before intubating a patient with long-standing disease?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Cervical spine disease and atlantoaxial joint instability (RA >10 years) — overextension of the neck during intubation may compromise the brainstem or spinal cord (p1612).

</details>

**8. At what glucocorticoid doses, and with what relative infection risks, did Medicare beneficiaries over 75 with RA show increased serious-infection rates?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

≤5 mg, 5–10 mg, and >10 mg/day of glucocorticoid conferred 20%, 32%, and 47% increased risk of serious infection respectively, versus 13% in non-users (p1618).

</details>

**9. How is late-onset lupus (LOL) defined, and what fraction of all SLE cases does it represent?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Onset of symptoms after age 55; it represents approximately 10% of all lupus cases (p1619).

</details>

**10. How does the female:male ratio of LOL compare with younger-onset lupus?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

LOL has a female:male ratio of about 4:1, versus about 9:1 in younger-onset lupus (p1621).

</details>

**11. When was belimumab first approved, and when was its indication expanded to lupus nephritis?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

FDA-approved in 2011 as the first lupus drug in its class (anti-BAFF); indication expanded to active lupus nephritis in 2021 (p1621–1622).

</details>

**12. What are the diagnostic thresholds for a positive Schirmer test and a positive minor salivary gland biopsy in Sjögren syndrome?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Schirmer test: less than 5 mm of filter paper wet after 5 minutes. Minor salivary gland biopsy: more than one focus (an aggregate of ≥50 lymphocytes) per 4 mm² of tissue (p1622).

</details>

**13. How much more likely are Sjögren syndrome patients to develop lymphoma compared to matched controls?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

About 44 times more likely (vs age-, sex- and race-matched controls); the risk may be higher if anti-Ro or anti-La positive (p1626).

</details>

**14. What dose thresholds define higher risk for hydralazine-induced lupus (HIL)?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Risk is dose-dependent and highest in patients taking more than 200 mg/day or with a cumulative dose over 100 g (p1625).

</details>

**15. What is the most common rheumatic syndrome associated with malignancy, and which cancer most often causes it?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy — almost always caused by metastatic lung cancer in the Western world (p1625).

</details>
