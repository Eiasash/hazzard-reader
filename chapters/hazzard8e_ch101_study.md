# Chapter 101 Study: Rheumatoid Arthritis and Other Autoimmune Diseases

## RA — definition & classification

<span data-exam-page="1608"></span>

- 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria (**Table 101-1**): joint involvement + serology + acute-phase reactants + duration; **score > 6** = definite RA (p1608)
  > Current practice (not the book): the original 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria require a score of ≥6/10 (a score of exactly 6 qualifies) — Aletaha et al., Arthritis Rheum 2010, Table 3.
- Criteria cohorts had mean age **46–58 yr** — usefulness for diagnosing **LORA (late-onset RA, >60 yr)** is unvalidated (p1607)
- Anti-CCP: **70–78%** sensitive, **88–96%** specific for RA — more specific than IgM RF, and unlike RF does not rise with normal aging (p1608)

## RA — epidemiology

- US/Western prevalence **~0.8%**; **0.2–0.3%** China/Japan/Greece/Africa; up to **7%** in some American-Indian/Alaska Native populations (p1608)
- Women affected **2–3×** more than men overall, but the **female predominance narrows in late-onset disease** (p1608) — LORA occurs with roughly equal M:F (p1610)
- Peak incidence in the **6th–8th decades** (p1608); RF prevalence rises with age to **~30% by age 90** (p1610)

## LORA clinical pattern (Table 101-2) (p1610)

<span data-exam-page="1610"></span>

- Acute onset, **oligoarticular (2–6 joints)**, large/proximal joints, systemic symptoms (weight loss), absent nodules, sicca common, high ESR, often RF-negative
- **RS3PE** syndrome: remitting seronegative symmetric synovitis with pitting edema; older men **2–3:1**, negative RF/ANA, responds to low-dose prednisone (p1610–1611)
- In one small study, anti-CCP **65%** positive in LORA vs **0%** in PMR or healthy older adults — may be useful to separate LORA from PMR (p1611)

## RA — evaluation

- ACR functional class I–IV (**Table 101-3**) (p1611)
- >10 yr RA duration → risk of **cervical spine/atlantoaxial instability** — assess before intubation (p1612)
- **Vectra DA** is a 12-protein blood test for RA disease activity. Hazzard describes it as US FDA-approved and as a complement to clinical judgment (p1612).
  > Current practice (not the book): Labcorp identifies Vectra as its laboratory-developed test and states that it lacks FDA clearance or approval. [Labcorp sample report, test 504965, 2022](https://files.labcorp.com/testmenu-d8/sample_reports/504965.pdf).
- Rheumatoid nodules in up to **40%** of seropositive White patients; pleuritis/pericarditis in **20–30%** (usually asymptomatic) (p1613)

## RA — pharmacotherapy (Table 101-4, Table 101-5)

- Methotrexate monotherapy = first-line for DMARD-naïve moderate-high activity, started **without short- or long-term glucocorticoids** (2021 ACR guideline) (p1615, p1616)
- Hydroxychloroquine dose **5 mg/kg**; **annual ocular screening**, preferably with OCT — avoid in macular degeneration/inoperable dense cataracts (p1614)

> Current practice (not the book): Use actual body weight when calculating hydroxychloroquine dose and generally stay at or below 5 mg/kg/day. Baseline retinal assessment and subsequent screening depend on duration, kidney function and other toxicity risks. OCT and wide-pattern fundus autofluorescence are core screening methods in the updated recommendations. [AAO hydroxychloroquine retinopathy recommendations, 2025 revision](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41232611/).
- Biologic/tsDMARD added if not at target on max-tolerated methotrexate, preferred over triple csDMARD therapy (p1615, p1616)

> Current practice (not the book): US labeling reserves tofacitinib, baricitinib and upadacitinib for patients with an inadequate response or intolerance to at least one TNF blocker. The boxed warnings address major cardiovascular events, cancer, thrombosis and death. Discuss these risks before treatment, particularly in older adults with cardiovascular risk, a smoking history or malignancy risk. [FDA JAK-inhibitor safety communication, 2021](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/janus-kinase-jak-inhibitors-drug-safety-communication-fda-requires-warnings-about-increased-risk).

## RA — biologics safety & steroids

- CONSIDER mnemonic for biologic safety in older adults (**Table 101-6**): Comorbidities, Opportunistic infections, Novel presentation of side effects, Screening, Immunization, Drug dosing and interactions, Evaluate, Remaining lifespan (p1618)
- In Medicare beneficiaries with RA **older than 75**, Hazzard reports **20%, 32% and 47% increased risk** of serious infection with glucocorticoid doses of **≤5, >5–10 and >10 mg/day**, respectively, and **13% risk** without glucocorticoids (printed wording, p1618).
  > Current practice (not the book): in the source (Medicare RA patients ≥75 y on stable DMARDs) 12.8/20.0/32.0/46.8 are crude hospitalized-infection rates per 100 person-years, not % increased risk; adjusted HRs vs no glucocorticoid 1.31, 1.74, 2.23 — George et al., Ann Intern Med 2020, Table 3.

<span data-exam-page="1617"></span>

- TB screening (skin test or IGRA + CXR) required before anticytokine therapy; anti-TNF associated with **~4×** malignancy rate in one meta-analysis (p1617)

<span data-exam-page="1619"></span>

- Immunization: review the history and ideally give all appropriate vaccines before DMARDs and biologics (particularly rituximab). Influenza and pneumococcal vaccines are given as in the general population. Short-term discontinuation of methotrexate for **2 weeks** after influenza vaccination may improve the vaccine response without a significant increase in RA activity; live vaccines are currently recommended against on biologic therapy (p1619)

## Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)

- **Late-onset lupus (LOL)**: onset after **age 55**, ~**10%** of all lupus cases (p1619)
- UK GPRD: peak incidence in women **50–54 yr**, in men **70–74 yr** (p1619)
- 2019 EULAR/ACR criteria (**Table 101-7**) use **ANA ≥1:80** as entry criterion (p1620)

<span data-exam-page="1621"></span>

- Patients with LOL may be more prone to cytopenias, serositis and interstitial pneumonitis than younger-onset patients (p1621)
- LOL diagnostic delay averages **2–3 years**; female:male ratio **4:1** in LOL vs **9:1** in younger-onset lupus (p1621)
- LOL symptom frequencies (**Table 101-8**): arthritis/arthralgia **60%**, rash **47%**, cytopenias **45%**, interstitial pneumonitis **41%**, serositis **32%**, glomerulonephritis only **24%** — milder, less classic disease than YOL (p1621)
- **Belimumab** (anti-BAFF) was the first lupus drug in its class to receive FDA approval in **2011**. Hazzard dates the expanded indication for active lupus nephritis to **2021**. Voclosporin, an oral calcineurin inhibitor, also received FDA approval for adult active lupus nephritis with background immunosuppression, with its role in older patients untested (pp1621–1622).
  > Current practice (not the book): the US supplemental approval for belimumab in adult active lupus nephritis was issued in **December 2020**. [FDA approval letter, 2020](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2020/125370Orig1s073_761043Orig1s013ltr.pdf).

## Sjögren syndrome

- Second commonest autoimmune disease after RA; prevalence **0.04–4.8%**; peak onset **4th–5th decade**, **9:1** F:M (p1622)
- Schirmer test positive if **<5 mm** wetting in **5 min**; minor salivary gland biopsy positive if **>1 focus (≥50 lymphocytes) per 4 mm²**; normal saliva production **≥1.5 mL/15 min** (p1622)
  > Current practice (not the book): 2016 ACR/EULAR primary Sjögren criteria use Schirmer ≤5 mm/5 min and focus score ≥1 focus/4 mm² — Shiboski et al., Arthritis Rheumatol 2017.
- Extraglandular disease in **~60%** (**Table 101-9**) — nonerosive arthritis/arthralgia is commonest (p1622–1623)

<span data-exam-page="1623"></span>

- Dry eye: artificial tears remain the mainstay, preservative-free for long-term use; topical low-dose cyclosporine can be used; punctal occlusion reduces tear drainage. **Anti-TNFs are not recommended** for sicca symptoms in primary Sjögren syndrome. For musculoskeletal symptoms, hydroxychloroquine is considered first line (p1623)
- Sjögren patients are **44×** more likely to develop lymphoma than matched controls, almost always B-cell, often t(14;18) (p1626)

## Drug-induced lupus (DIL) (Table 101-10)

- >100 drugs implicated; incidence **~15,000–20,000/yr**, **30,000–50,000** affected in the US (p1624–1625)
- Procainamide: **1/3** of patients on it >1 yr develop symptoms, almost all ANA+ by 2 yr; hydralazine: **<20%** develop DIL, risk dose-dependent (**>200 mg/day** or **>100 g** cumulative) (p1625)
- >90% of PIL/HIL patients have antihistone antibodies; IFN-α causes autoimmunity in up to **19%** of treated patients (p1625)

## Rheumatic syndromes & cancer

- **Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy** = most common malignancy-associated rheumatic syndrome, almost always from metastatic **lung cancer** in the Western world (p1625)
- Methotrexate-associated lymphoma: **~50%** harbor EBV genome, often regresses on drug cessation (p1626)
- Azathioprine or cyclosporin A in RA → **2×** lymphoma risk; renal transplant on cyclosporin A → **50-fold** increased NHL risk (p1626)
- Aromatase inhibitors: as many as **half** of women get subjective joint achiness or pain, rarely frank arthritis (p1626–1627)

## COVID-19 and rheumatic disease (Table 101-11)

- Historical vaccine schedule in this edition: mRNA vaccines were preferred to J&J, and an extra dose at least 28 days after completing the two-dose mRNA primary series was advised for immunosuppressed patients (p1627)
- Rituximab: vaccinate **2–4 weeks before** next anticipated dose; most other agents held **1–2 weeks** post-vaccination if disease activity allows — but the Task Force reached **no consensus** on interrupting TNF/IL-6R/IL-1R/IL-17/IL-12,23/IL-23 inhibitors around vaccination (Table 101-11, p1628)

> Current practice (not the book): For COVID-19 vaccination, ACR guidance timing varies by drug and disease activity. The panel did not reach consensus on holding TNF inhibitors or other cytokine inhibitors. Coordinate rituximab timing separately; a class-wide pause is inappropriate. [ACR COVID-19 vaccination guidance, version 5, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36345691/).

## Recall

**1. What are the sensitivity and specificity of anti-CCP antibodies for RA?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-2"><summary>Answer</summary>

70–78% sensitive, 88–96% specific — and unlike RF, anti-CCP incidence does not rise with normal aging (p1608).

</details>

**2. How does the male:female ratio of RA differ between typical (younger-onset) RA and LORA?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-3"><summary>Answer</summary>

Typical RA affects women 2–3× more than men, but the female predominance narrows in late-onset disease (p1608) — LORA occurs with roughly equal frequency in men and women (p1610).

</details>

**3. What joint pattern and demographic features characterize LORA (Table 101-2)?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-4"><summary>Answer</summary>

Acute-onset oligoarticular disease (2–6 joints), involvement of large/proximal joints, systemic symptoms (e.g., weight loss), absence of rheumatoid nodules, common sicca symptoms, and a roughly 1:1 male:female ratio (p1610).

</details>

**4. What is RS3PE syndrome and how does it typically respond to treatment?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-5"><summary>Answer</summary>

Remitting seronegative symmetric synovitis with pitting edema — affects older men (2–3:1), RF/ANA negative, high ESR, and generally responds well to low-dose prednisone (p1610–1611).

</details>

**5. At what glucocorticoid doses, and with what relative infection risks, did Medicare beneficiaries over 75 with RA show increased serious-infection rates?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-8"><summary>Answer</summary>

For Medicare beneficiaries with RA older than 75, p1618 reports **20%, 32% and 47% increased risk** of serious infection at **≤5, >5–10 and >10 mg/day**, respectively, and **13% risk** without glucocorticoids (printed wording).
> Current practice (not the book): in the source (Medicare RA patients ≥75 y on stable DMARDs) 12.8/20.0/32.0/46.8 are crude hospitalized-infection rates per 100 person-years, not % increased risk; adjusted HRs vs no glucocorticoid 1.31, 1.74, 2.23 — George et al., Ann Intern Med 2020, Table 3.

</details>

**6. How is late-onset lupus (LOL) defined, and what fraction of all SLE cases does it represent?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-9"><summary>Answer</summary>

Onset of symptoms after age 55; it represents approximately 10% of all lupus cases (p1619).

</details>

**7. How does the female:male ratio of LOL compare with younger-onset lupus?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-10"><summary>Answer</summary>

LOL has a female:male ratio of about 4:1, versus about 9:1 in younger-onset lupus (p1621).

</details>

**8. When was belimumab first approved, and when was its indication expanded to lupus nephritis?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-11"><summary>Answer</summary>

Hazzard dates initial FDA approval to **2011** and the expanded active lupus nephritis indication to **2021** (pp1621–1622).
> Current practice (not the book): the US supplemental approval was issued in **December 2020**. [FDA approval letter, 2020](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2020/125370Orig1s073_761043Orig1s013ltr.pdf).

</details>

**9. What are the diagnostic thresholds for a positive Schirmer test and a positive minor salivary gland biopsy in Sjögren syndrome?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-12"><summary>Answer</summary>

Schirmer test: less than 5 mm of filter paper wet after 5 minutes. Minor salivary gland biopsy: more than one focus (an aggregate of ≥50 lymphocytes) per 4 mm² of tissue (p1622).
> Current practice (not the book): 2016 ACR/EULAR primary Sjögren criteria use Schirmer ≤5 mm/5 min and focus score ≥1 focus/4 mm² — Shiboski et al., Arthritis Rheumatol 2017.

</details>

**10. What dose thresholds define higher risk for hydralazine-induced lupus (HIL)?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-14"><summary>Answer</summary>

Risk is dose-dependent and highest in patients taking more than 200 mg/day or with a cumulative dose over 100 g (p1625).

</details>

**11. What is the most common rheumatic syndrome associated with malignancy, and which cancer most often causes it?**

<details data-recall-key="101s:recall-15"><summary>Answer</summary>

Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy — almost always caused by metastatic lung cancer in the Western world (p1625).

</details>
