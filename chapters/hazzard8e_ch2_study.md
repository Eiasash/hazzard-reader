# Chapter 2 Study: Demography and Epidemiology

## Summary

**Population aging and its measurement**
- Improved survival and declining birth rates reshape age distributions; population aging differs across countries and historical periods (p23-25).
- Interpret remaining life expectancy at a specified age separately from life expectancy at birth; survival curves reflect changing mortality across the lifespan (p25-26).
- Distinguish age effects, period effects shared during an event, and birth-cohort effects from shared historical exposure (p26-27).
- The dependency ratio compares conventional working/nonworking age groups; it does not establish an individual's actual work or economic dependence (p27).
- US older populations are becoming more diverse. Aggregate survival gains coexist with racial, ethnic and socioeconomic differences in morbidity and disability (p27).

**Disease burden, function and cognition**
- Hypertension is the most common chronic condition among older Medicare beneficiaries; chronic diseases commonly coexist rather than appearing in isolation (p29-31).
- In **2018**, multiple chronic conditions affected **64%** of adults **65 or older**, versus **33%** at **45-64**. Comorbidity relates to an index disease; multimorbidity describes coexisting conditions (p31).
- Sensory loss contributes to impaired function and independence; utilization and mortality statistics alone do not describe the full burden (p32-33).
- Disability concerns difficulty with daily activities; ADLs capture basic self-care, IADLs more complex independent living. Performance tests add information (p33-34).
- Disablement links pathology, impairment, functional limitation and disability; personal and environmental factors modify the pathway. Disability may develop gradually or abruptly and can improve (p36-37).
- Cognitive and physical function interact. Screening instruments differ by domain and sensitivity; education and ceiling effects can complicate interpretation (p40).

**Modifiable context**
- Biomarkers may reflect disease processes or exposures, but clinical interpretation requires context rather than treating every age association as causation (p40-42).
- Physical activity, smoking, alcohol, nutrition and other behaviors influence later-life health; socioeconomic resources shape opportunities to adopt them (p42-43).
- Marginalization, discrimination, neighborhood design and social connections influence disability and survival. Address both individual risks and environmental barriers to participation (p43-44).
