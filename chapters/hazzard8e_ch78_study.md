# Chapter 78 Study: Peripheral Vascular Disease

## Summary

**Peripheral arterial disease**
- PAD affects up to **20%** of people over **70** and commonly reflects atherosclerotic narrowing from the infrarenal aorta to tibial vessels (p1209).
- Vascular claudication occurs at a relatively reproducible walking distance and subsides with rest. Spinal stenosis symptoms have more variable onset and may improve with forward leaning (p1210).
- Examine distal lesions and pulses, but assess motor/sensory function when acute or severe ischemia is suspected; foot viability is not determined by pulses alone (p1210).
- ABI is the highest ankle systolic pressure divided by the highest brachial systolic pressure. Further imaging should fit the specialist's diagnostic/intervention plan (p1210–1211).

**Management & drugs**
- Smoking cessation and supervised walking are central. Typical exercise sessions last **30 minutes**, repeated three times weekly, walking as far beyond pain onset as safely tolerated (p1211).
- Antiplatelet treatment and a high-potency statin address vascular risk. Cilostazol may improve claudication distance; use caution with heart failure (p1211).
- Consider claudication intervention for lifestyle limitation after an exercise trial; progression to limb loss is not inevitable (p1209–1210).

**Carotid disease & aneurysm**
- Carotid plaque can embolize and cause stroke; maximize antiplatelet, lipid and risk-factor management, including smoking cessation (p1212–1213).
- The chapter generally favors endarterectomy over stenting in older patients; stenting has specific indications and is not routinely indicated for asymptomatic older patients (p1209–1210).
- AAA means over **50%** vessel-diameter enlargement, generally above **3 cm** in men. It may remain silent until expansion or rupture (p1214).
- Repair is generally considered above **5–5.5 cm**; endovascular repair has short-term advantages, but long-term outcomes can resemble open repair (p1210).
- Endovascular repair still requires long-term imaging follow-up; medical management includes smoking cessation and control of comorbid vascular risks (p1210, p1215).

**Venous disease & exam traps**
- Chronic venous insufficiency results from venous hypertension, usually incompetent valves; elevation and compression are the mainstays (p1216–1217).
- AAA does not automatically prohibit aerobic activity: the chapter encourages activity and notes no rupture-prevention benefit demonstrated for doxycycline (p1215).
