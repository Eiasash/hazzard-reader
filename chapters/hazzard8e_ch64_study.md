# Chapter 64 Study: Traumatic Brain Injury and Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy

## Summary

**TBI & later-life disease**
- TBI is heterogeneous: characterize severity, loss of consciousness, amnesia, frequency, injury mechanism and associated hemorrhage or other damage (p997).
- TBI of varying severity is associated with later dementia, but the underlying pathology is not established as simply Alzheimer disease (p997).
- A cited study found **44%** greater subsequent Parkinson diagnosis risk after TBI than after nonbrain trauma in middle-aged/older patients; severity and frequency influenced risk (p999).

**Chronic traumatic encephalopathy**
- CTE is a primary tauopathy associated with repetitive head impacts, including concussive and subconcussive exposure (p997, p999).
- Exposures include contact sports, military blast and repeated physical abuse; the amount/duration of exposure matters (p997, p999).
- The characteristic lesion is irregular perivascular phosphorylated tau in neurons and neurites at cortical sulcal depths (p997, p1004).
- Purely astrocytic perivascular tau, including age-related tau astrogliopathy, does not meet the pathological definition of CTE (p1004).
- Early CTE need not contain amyloid; amyloid deposition may appear with aging alongside the tau pathology (p1004).

**Assessment & progression**
- Behavioral, cognitive, dementia and motor presentations are nonspecific; no imaging, laboratory or cognitive test definitively diagnoses CTE during life (p997).
- Definitive diagnosis requires postmortem neuropathology; clinical symptoms or exposure history alone cannot establish it (p1005).
- Pathology generally becomes more extensive over time, involving medial temporal and deep brain structures; some individuals have an indolent course even at advanced age (p1004–1005).
- Coexisting disease can contribute to symptom progression; TBI may also cause axonal loss, white-matter degeneration and persistent inflammation (p998–999, p1005).

**Clinical implications & exam traps**
- Do not equate all post-TBI dementia with either Alzheimer pathology or CTE; distinguish single injury from repetitive impact exposure (p997–999).
- Selected brain-bank case frequencies are not population prevalence estimates; the chapter notes that prevalence in neurodegenerative brain banks remains uncertain (p998).
- The chapter emphasizes diagnostic uncertainty and biomarker development; it does not provide an established CTE-specific drug regimen (p1005).
