# Chapter 83 Study: Kidney Diseases

## Summary

### CKD — Definition & Diagnosis
- CKD criteria: eGFR **<60 mL/min/1.73 m²** (normal young-adult GFR **~125**; **<15** = kidney failure), albuminuria (ACR **>30 mg/g**), hematuria (any degree), urinary casts, or imaging abnormality — any persisting **>3 months** (p1278).
- CKD is usually asymptomatic until GFR falls to roughly **15–20 mL/min/1.73 m²** (p1278).
- US CKD prevalence **~11.5%** overall (4.8% stages 1–2, 6.7% stages 3–5) but **47%** in adults **>70y** (p1278).

### CKD — Management
- BP target in CKD: **SBP 120–130**, **DBP <90** (p1279); goal of **<130/80** for diabetes + CKD (p1286).
- ACEI/ARB (RAS blockade) is the mainstay in diabetes when DN is suspected; **do not combine ACEI+ARB** (↑ complications) (p1279).
- SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce CKD-progression risk in diabetics; benefit in nondiabetics is still emerging (p1279–1280).
- Statins (± ezetimibe) recommended once eGFR **<60** (p1280).
- Low-protein diet (0.5–0.6 g/kg/d) or very-low (0.3–0.4 g/kg/d) — some evidence it may reduce progression to ESKD in **CKD stage 4–5**; in nondiabetic CKD stage ≤3 it does not appear to reduce progression (p1280).
- Discuss dialysis preferences once eGFR falls **<~20** (p1280).
- Nephrology referral triggers (KCP list, p1278): AKI, red cell casts, refractory HTN, K⁺ abnormalities, recurrent/extensive stones, hereditary kidney disease, **CKD stage 4–5**, severely increased albuminuria. K/DIGO's own restated list (p1290) phrases the GFR trigger as **GFR <30** instead.

### AKI
- Classified by urine output: nonoliguric **>400 mL/day**, oliguric **100–400**, anuric **<100** — nonoliguric has the better prognosis (p1280).
- Categorized prerenal / intrarenal / postrenal (Table 83-1, p1281–1282).
- NSAID use **doubles** AKI risk in adults **>65** (p1282).
- CI-AKI (contrast-induced) risk factors: old age, CKD, diabetes, heart failure, volume depletion, other nephrotoxins (p1282).

### Glomerulonephritis / RPGN
- IgA nephropathy is the most common primary GN worldwide; **30–40%** progress to ESKD within 20 years; ESKD incidence is almost **2×** higher in patients **older than 50** (p1283).
- Postinfectious GN (PIGN): hematuria + proteinuria **10–14 days** post-infection; hypocomplementemia in **>70%**; **~50%** need acute dialysis; only **~20%** achieve complete recovery, **~30%** progress to ESKD (p1283).
- RPGN classification — Table 83-2 (p1284).
- Corticosteroids are the mainstay; cyclophosphamide for crescentic/proliferative disease or life-threatening complications (p1284–1285); rituximab is roughly **equivalent potency** to cyclophosphamide (p1285).

### Nephrotic Syndrome
- Definition: proteinuria **>3 g/day** + hypoalbuminemia + edema + dyslipidemia (p1285).
- Most common biopsy findings in older adults with NS: membranous nephropathy (MN) + minimal change disease (MCD) + amyloidosis = **~60%** combined — MCD **~15%**, MN **~30–40%** of biopsies (p1285).
- Amyloidosis found on **~10%** of biopsies done for presumed idiopathic NS (p1285).
- Treatment: RAS blockade for proteinuria; sodium restriction **<2 g/day** + loop diuretics for edema (p1286).

### Diabetic Nephropathy (DN)
- **~30%** of diabetics develop DN in their lifetime; DN is the leading cause of NS and dialysis-ESKD in the US (p1286).
- Accelerated kidney-function loss once eGFR falls **<45 mL/min/1.73 m²** (p1286).
- ACEI/ARB, SGLT2i, and GLP-1 RA all delay CKD→ESKD progression; BP goal **<130/80** (p1286).

### Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN)
- **~80%** of older patients show partial/complete recovery within 6 months (p1287).
- Most cases in older adults are drug-induced (antibiotics, PPIs, NSAIDs, diuretics); stopping the offending drug is the key intervention (p1287).
- Corticosteroids improve outcomes if started within **14 days** of first symptoms (p1287, main text).
- Rare: airway involvement can cause bronchospasm (p1287).

### Renovascular Disease
- Prevalence **2–3%** of general hypertensive population; up to **40%** of refractory hypertension (p1287).
- Atherosclerosis accounts for almost **90%** of cases in older adults, fibrous dysplasia the rest (p1287); other causes — Table 83-3 (p1288).
- Duplex ultrasound sensitivity/specificity: low-to-mid **90%** range (p1288).
- Renal vein renin ratio **≥1.5** between kidneys predicts a beneficial BP response to revascularization; failure to lateralize does *not* predict a negative response (p1289).
- CORAL trial (**947** patients): no benefit of revascularization vs. medical management (p1289).
- Up to **50%** of stenoses worsen over time, at up to **5%/year** (p1289).

### ESKD — Epidemiology & Referral
- ESKD/kidney failure = GFR **<15 mL/min/1.73 m²** (p1289).
- **~10–13%** of adults in North America, Europe, and Asia are estimated to have some form of CKD (p1290).
- Historic untreated CKD decline rate **~7–10 mL/min/year** (p1290).
- **~60%** of US ESKD patients have nausea/vomiting at the time dialysis is initiated (p1291).

### Renal Replacement Therapy Choices
- Conventional hemodialysis: **3×/week**, **~4 h**/session; access options are AV fistula (best outcomes), AV graft, or temporary catheter (high bacteremia rates) (p1291–1292).
- AV fistula matures over **~3 months** before it's usable (p1292).
- Peritoneal dialysis: continuous cyclic or nocturnal PD preferred in long-term care (p1293).
- Among patients **>75** placed on a kidney-transplant waitlist, **<40%** receive a transplant within **5 years** (p1293).
- A time-limited dialysis trial is typically predetermined at **4–6 weeks** (p1293).

### ESKD Complications
- Anemia: typically develops around GFR **~35 mL/min**; normochromic, normocytic (p1294). Conservative hemoglobin target **~11.5** (printed "mg/dL" in the book — clinically hemoglobin is measured in g/dL; read the number, not the stated unit) (p1294).
- Functional iron deficiency in ESKD: ferritin **<500 ng/mL** or iron saturation **<30%** (p1294).
- Cardiovascular death risk on dialysis: **10–100×** the general-population risk (p1294).
- Secondary hyperparathyroidism: PTH begins rising by GFR **~40 mL/min** (p1295).
- Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) supplementation for malnutrition: **50 mg/day** (p1295).
- Pruritus affects up to **40%** of ESKD patients; low-dose gabapentin reduces uremic pruritus; **avoid** diphenhydramine/hydroxyzine (anticholinergic burden) (p1296).

### Prognosis & End of Life
- **1-year survival** for octogenarians/nonagenarians after starting dialysis: **50%** per the chapter's own Key Clinical Points (p1278) — see book contradiction below.
- **87%** of nursing-home residents died or had decreased functional status within 1 year of starting hemodialysis (p1296).
- Hospice use is **5-fold lower** among hemodialysis patients than among older non-dialysis patients (p1297).
- The "surprise question" (would you be surprised if this patient died in 6 months?), combined with albumin, age, dementia, and peripheral vascular disease, is a validated mortality predictor (p1296).

### Drugs (name — dose/target — key cautions)
- **Phosphate binders** (calcium acetate, sevelamer, lanthanum carbonate) — for hyperphosphatemia not controlled by diet alone (p1280).
- **Calcitriol** (active vitamin D analog) — for PTH unresponsive to plain vitamin D supplementation; caution: hypercalcemia and hyperphosphatemia (p1280).
- **Pyridoxine** — 50 mg/day for malnutrition (p1295); **gabapentin** — low-dose, for uremic pruritus (p1296); **avoid** diphenhydramine/hydroxyzine in ESKD pruritus (anticholinergic burden) (p1296).

### Book contradiction (exam trap)
- **1-year dialysis survival for octogenarians/nonagenarians is given as two different numbers in the same chapter**: the opening Key Clinical Points box says **50%** (p1278), but the "Prognosis and Survival" section later states **54%** (p1296) for the identical statistic. Both are printed as-is; know which figure a question is quoting.

## Drill

**1. What eGFR, albuminuria, and duration criteria define CKD?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² (or albuminuria >30 mg/g, hematuria, urinary casts, or imaging abnormality), persisting more than 3 months (p1278).

</details>

**2. What fraction of US adults over age 70 have CKD by eGFR/albuminuria criteria?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

47%, versus about 11.5% in the general adult population (p1278).

</details>

**3. What blood pressure target is recommended for older adults with CKD?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Systolic 120–130 mm Hg, diastolic under 90 mm Hg (p1279).

</details>

**4. At what eGFR does statin therapy become specifically recommended in CKD, and is combining ACEI with ARB advised?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

eGFR under 60 mL/min/1.73 m². Combining ACEI and ARB is generally not advised — it increases complications (p1279–1280).

</details>

**5. How is AKI classified by urine output, and which category has the best prognosis?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Nonoliguric (>400 mL/day), oliguric (100–400 mL/day), anuric (<100 mL/day); nonoliguric AKI has the better prognosis (p1280).

</details>

**6. By how much does NSAID use raise AKI risk in adults over 65?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It doubles the risk (p1282).

</details>

**7. What proportion of IgA nephropathy patients progress to ESKD within 20 years, and how does age at onset affect ESKD risk?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

30–40% progress to ESKD within 20 years; ESKD incidence is almost twice as high in patients older than 50 (p1283).

</details>

**8. What three diagnoses account for about 60% of kidney-biopsy findings in older adults with nephrotic syndrome?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Membranous nephropathy, minimal change disease, and amyloidosis (p1285).

</details>

**9. At what eGFR does diabetic nephropathy show accelerated loss of kidney function, and what is the BP goal in diabetes plus CKD?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

eGFR below 45 mL/min/1.73 m²; BP goal under 130/80 mm Hg (p1286).

</details>

**10. What proportion of older patients with acute interstitial nephritis recover partially or completely within 6 months, and what is the single most important management step?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

About 80%; the most important step is stopping the offending drug (p1287).

</details>

**11. What proportion of renovascular disease in older adults is due to atherosclerosis, and what did the CORAL trial show about revascularization?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

About 90%; CORAL (947 patients) showed no benefit of revascularization over medical management (p1287, p1289).

</details>

**12. What renal vein renin ratio predicts a beneficial blood pressure response to revascularization?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A ratio of 1.5 or greater between the two renal veins (p1289).

</details>

**13. Around what GFR does renal anemia typically develop, and what type of anemia is it?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Around GFR 35 mL/min; a normochromic, normocytic anemia due to inadequate erythropoietin production (p1294).

</details>

**14. How much higher is the risk of cardiovascular death for a patient on dialysis compared to the general population?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

10 to 100 times higher (p1294).

</details>

**15. The chapter states two different 1-year survival rates for octogenarians/nonagenarians starting dialysis — what are they, and where does each appear?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

50% (Key Clinical Points, p1278) and 54% (Prognosis and Survival section, p1296) — the same statistic printed differently in two places in the same chapter.

</details>
