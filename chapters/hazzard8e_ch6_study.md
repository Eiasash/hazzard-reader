# Chapter 6 Study: Social Determinants of Health, Health Disparities, and Health Equity

> Excluded from the IMA required reading list (P005-2026); not cited in the 2020-2026 past papers.

## Summary

**Separate the concepts**
- Social determinants are conditions of daily life: education, economic stability, health-care access, neighborhood/environment and social/community context (p95-96).
- Health disparities are differences in outcomes driven by unjust or inequitable conditions. Health equity is an ongoing pursuit of optimal health for all, centering historically disenfranchised people (p97-98).
- Identity itself does not create disparities. Race is a social construct, not a biologic category; structural forces and discrimination shape exposure and opportunity (p98).
- Intersectionality considers overlapping identities and systems of disadvantage; avoid treating any group as homogeneous (p98, p100).

**History and the life course**
- Colonialism, slavery, segregation and later policies can have lasting health effects. Understand mistrust as potentially reflecting trust that institutions have not earned (p98-99).
- The developmental life-course view emphasizes sensitive periods; the structural view emphasizes cumulative, socially patterned exposures and weathering (p100).
- Older Indigenous, LGBT+, incarcerated, immigrant and rural populations may face distinct and intersecting barriers; individual assessment remains necessary (p99-100).

**Translate assessment into action**
- Ask about education, work, income, housing, transport, food, safety, social support and health-care access. Life-story prompts can make screening more meaningful (p101-103).
- Invite discussion of discrimination and prior care experiences; also recognize coping strategies, strengths and health-promoting behaviors (p101-102).
- Use the patient's terms and pronouns, accessible communication and appropriate language services. Cultural knowledge should not become stereotyping (p100, p102).
- Include family, friends and supports chosen by the patient; avoid restrictive definitions of family or imposing clinician preferences (p102).
- Organizational/policy approaches include workforce diversity, integrated health and community services, funding and evaluation of local outcomes (p102-103).
- Community services may meet nutritional, housing, counseling, safety and financial needs, but availability, quality and access vary; check actual local resources before referral (p104).
- Assess consequences of clinical decisions such as stopping driving, including isolation and transport access. Work with the patient to connect medical recommendations with practical support (p104-105).
