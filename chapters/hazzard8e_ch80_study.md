# Chapter 80 Study: Respiratory System and Selected Pulmonary Disorders

## Summary

**Aging & physiology**
- Lung function reflects lung tissue, airways, respiratory muscles, chest wall and cardiovascular function; normal decline need not reach symptomatic disease (p1229).
- Senescence limits tissue repair; a persistent senescence-associated secretory phenotype promotes inflammation and fibrosis (p1230).
- Fewer naive T cells, impaired ciliary clearance and altered extracellular matrix increase vulnerability to infection and stress (p1230).

**Assessment**
- Spirometry measures exhaled flow and volume; a lower FEV1/FVC ratio indicates greater obstruction. Spirometry alone cannot measure total lung volume (p1230).
- With aging, FEV1 falls more than FVC; the ratio declines. Opposing lung and chest-wall changes leave total lung capacity relatively constant (p1231).
- Diffusing capacity declines with capillary loss and ventilation–perfusion mismatch (p1231).
- IPF is more common after **60**; consider it with insidious progressive exertional dyspnea, cough and basal fine crackles (p1231).
- IPF usually reduces total lung capacity and diffusing capacity. Chest CT may show the usual interstitial pneumonia pattern: reticulation, honeycombing and traction bronchiectasis (p1231).

**Management & drugs**
- Refer suspected or confirmed IPF to pulmonology. Nintedanib and pirfenidone slow progression of restriction but do not reverse existing scarring; nausea and diarrhea may limit treatment (p1231).
- Antifibrotic initiation and continuation require individual decisions; immediate symptom or quality-of-life improvement may not occur. The chapter does not specify their doses (p1231).
- Avoid tobacco and pollution; address vaccination, healthy weight, cardiovascular disease and osteoporosis. Consider oxygen supplementation if hypoxic (p1229, p1232–1233).
- Consider pulmonary/cardiac rehabilitation after acute illness in patients with chronic cardiopulmonary disease; recovery may otherwise be slow and incomplete (p1233).

**Exam traps**
- Aging does not simply reduce every lung volume: total lung capacity remains relatively constant while airflow and diffusion decline (p1231).
- IPF is primarily fibrotic: the chapter centers treatment on antifibrotics rather than chronic immunosuppression; consider transplant evaluation for suitable patients (p1231).
