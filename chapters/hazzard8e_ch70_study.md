# Chapter 70 Study: Palliative Care Across Care Settings

## Summary

**Purpose & timing**
- Palliative care seeks quality of life through symptom relief, attention to psychological/spiritual distress and support for patients and families (p1077–1078).
- It can begin at diagnosis of serious or advanced illness, or with progressive frailty and cumulative disease burden; it is not limited to hospice (p1079, p1086).
- Geriatrics and palliative care share attention to multimorbidity, dementia, disability, caregivers and the person's community context (p1078).

**Assessment & care plan**
- Clarify values and preferences through advance care planning; address pain, nonpain symptoms, function, cognition and caregiver needs (p1078).
- Reassess needs as function and care setting change. An interprofessional team and consistent communication can support continuity (p1078–1079).
- Practical support includes adaptive devices, home modifications, caregiver education and access to medication and community resources (p1078, p1084).

**Across settings**
- Inpatient teams provide symptom expertise, prognostic support, family communication and links to community services (p1080).
- Outpatient clinics permit earlier relationships and follow-up after inpatient consultation; access still depends on mobility and available assistance (p1083).
- Home-based care can reach seriously ill patients who do not qualify for hospice. Completely homebound patients have approximately **40%** mortality over **2 years** in cited data (p1084).
- An ideal home model integrates primary and palliative care, respite, spiritual support and **24/7** clinician access for crises (p1084).
- Long-term-care barriers include prognostic uncertainty in noncancer illness, frequent transitions, staff turnover and reimbursement structures (p1085).

**Hospice & exam traps**
- Hospice is a holistic philosophy addressing medical, emotional, spiritual and family needs in life-limiting illness (p1085).
- Most older adults' late-life care occurs in the community, while specialist palliative access remains concentrated in hospitals and hospice (p1079).
- A hospital consultation alone does not create longitudinal care; transitions need shared goals, follow-up and continued caregiver support (p1078–1079, p1086).
- Uncertain prognosis and ongoing restorative treatment do not erase palliative needs; the chapter advocates integrating care earlier across the trajectory (p1084–1086).
