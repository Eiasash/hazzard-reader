# Chapter 20 Study: The Role of Social Workers

## Summary

**What social workers contribute**
- Social workers assess social problems, support coping, mediate conflict and obtain resources for patients and families; geriatric expertise is not universal among social workers (p277).
- Direct work includes counseling, therapy, education and groups. Support groups can help caregivers understand illness and cope with its effects (p277-278).
- Linkage work connects patients with services and addresses lack of information, access barriers, stigma and fears about accepting help (p278).
- Case management assesses needs, coordinates delivery and reassesses whether services remain useful as circumstances change (p278-279).

**Match help to the setting**
- Home care addresses adaptation, caregiving, safety, finances, medication misuse, abuse and end-of-life issues; services vary across agencies (p279).
- Community agencies combine direct support, mental health care, education, service development and policy roles (p280).
- Nursing homes and assisted living require psychosocial assessment, adjustment support, resident advocacy and work with families and staff (p280).
- Hospital discharge planning starts early; assess practical capacity and connect the patient with appropriate community or institutional support (p280-281).
- Hospice/palliative social work addresses loss, family communication, psychosocial and spiritual needs, transitions and bereavement (p281).

**Vulnerability and assessment**
- Explore culture and within-group differences rather than assuming shared needs. Poverty, immigration/language barriers, discrimination and sexual or gender identity can affect access and trust (p281-283).
- In the United States, more than **50%** of people living with HIV are **50 or older**; late testing may leave newly diagnosed older adults facing more immediate challenges (p283).
- Abuse may require repeated contacts to detect. Reporting duties and procedures vary locally; assess risk and work with protective services when indicated (p283-284).
- Cognitive assessment includes function, living conditions, family dynamics, resources and legal/advance-planning needs; provide stage-appropriate dementia support (p284).
- Mental illness and substance misuse can impair independence. Do not automatically attribute symptoms to age, dementia or depression; consider isolation, pain and losses (p285).
