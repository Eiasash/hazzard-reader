# Chapter 35 Study: Sexuality, Sexual Function, and the Aging Woman

## Summary

**Ask without assumptions**
- Sexuality can remain important with age, illness or absence of a partner; use a biopsychosocial approach that includes partner health and context (p503-504).
- Invite discussion privately and nonjudgmentally; ask about partners, pleasure, concerns and the effect of illness or treatment (p516-517).
- A sexual problem is not automatically a disorder: distress, personal goals and relationship context matter (p508, p516).
- Respect sexual and gender diversity, privacy and consensual relationships in long-term care; cognitive impairment does not itself erase sexuality (p505-507).
- Assess STI exposure and prevention needs, and ask sensitively about trauma, coercion and partner violence (p509-510).

**Evaluate the concern**
- Separate desire/arousal, orgasm and pain/penetration concerns; determine lifelong versus acquired and situational versus generalized patterns (p516-520, p523).
- Review physical and mental health, medicines, surgery, cancer treatment and relationship factors (p515, p518, p521).
- Menopausal tissue changes can contribute to dryness and pain; examination can also educate the patient about anatomy and findings (p513, p521-522).
- PLISSIT progresses from permission and limited information to specific suggestions and intensive therapy when needed (p518).

**Tailor treatment**
- Lubricants reduce friction; moisturizers, treatment of underlying disease, pelvic therapy and graded dilation may help selected women (p522-523).
- Psychotherapy, couples/sex therapy and adapted sexual-skills education can address relevant concerns (p522-523).
- The book lists conjugated estrogen, ospemifene and intravaginal prasterone for postmenopausal sexual symptoms; treatment must match the specific problem (p523).
- Testosterone is used off-label in some women; adverse effects may be irreversible and safety in older women is unestablished. DHEA reviews have not shown efficacy (p523).
- Flibanserin is taken daily orally and bremelanotide by pre-encounter injection; the chapter states neither was approved for postmenopausal women (p523).
- Older women are poorly represented in treatment trials; preserve that uncertainty when discussing benefits and harms (p522-523).
